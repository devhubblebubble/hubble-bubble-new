A one-year UK master's can still be worth it if it leads to a job you want and you can afford it. As of October 2026, the Graduate visa lasts 2 years if you apply by 31 December 2026, and 18 months from 1 January 2027. To stay longer, a Skilled Worker job usually needs to pay at least £41,700 a year.

## How long is a UK master's?

Most full-time taught master's take one year. The UK framework for degrees, published by QAA, says most master's outcomes come from "study equivalent to at least one full-time calendar year".

Universities say the same. Aberystwyth University, for example, says a taught postgraduate programme "should take one year (full-time) or two years (part-time)". In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, a master's is typically at least 180 credits.

Some courses take longer. At the University of East Anglia, for example, a [master's with a placement year](https://www.uea.ac.uk/study/postgraduate/masters-with-placement) takes two years, with the placement in the second year.

## What are the pros and cons of one year?

This is advice, based on how a one-year course works.

The pros:

- You pay fees and living costs for about one year.
- You are back in work sooner.
- If you already know your field, a year of focused study can be enough.

The cons:

- There is little time for an internship or placement.
- You need to apply for jobs while you are still studying. Start in your first term.
- If you are changing career, one year is a short time to build evidence. Read [can a master's help you change career?](/blog/career-change-masters/)

## What changes for the Graduate visa in 2027?

The Graduate visa lets you stay in the UK after your course. You can look for work and work in most jobs.

How long you get depends on when you apply:

| When you apply | Length of Graduate visa |
|---|---|
| On or before 31 December 2026 | 2 years |
| On or after 1 January 2027 | 18 months |
| After a PhD or other doctorate | 3 years |

If your one-year course ends in 2027, you will apply in 2027, so plan for 18 months. You cannot extend a Graduate visa, but you may be able to switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa.

You must normally have studied in the UK for your whole course. For a course longer than 12 months, at least 12 months in the UK is enough. For the details, read [UK Graduate visa: 2 years or 18 months?](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-or-2-years/)

## How much does a one-year UK master's cost?

Tuition depends on the university and the course. For a full breakdown, read [how much it costs to study in the UK](/blog/cost-of-studying-in-the-uk/).

The visa fees and money rules are set by the government. As of October 2026:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Student visa fee, from outside the UK | £558 |
| Immigration Health Surcharge for students | £776 a year |
| Money to show, course in London | £1,529 a month, for up to 9 months |
| Money to show, course elsewhere | £1,171 a month, for up to 9 months |
| Graduate visa fee | £937 |
| Graduate visa health surcharge | Usually £1,035 a year |

From 30 November 2026, the money to show rises to £1,570 a month in London and £1,203 a month elsewhere. Applications made before that date are decided under the rules in force on 29 November 2026.

You must hold the money for at least 28 days in a row. You also need enough money for your course fees for one academic year (up to 9 months). Read [UK Student visa 2026: cost, money and timeline](/blog/uk-student-visa-guide/).

## What do you need for a work visa after that?

To stay longer, most graduates need a Skilled Worker visa. You need a confirmed job offer from an employer approved by the Home Office, and the job must qualify.

- **The job's level.** Since 22 July 2025, new applicants usually need a job listed as higher skilled. The Immigration Rules describe these jobs as skilled to RQF level 6 or above, the level of a bachelor's degree with honours.
- **Medium skilled jobs.** These qualify only if they are on the immigration salary list or the temporary shortage list.
- **The salary.** As of October 2026, the standard minimum is £41,700 a year, or the going rate for the job if that is higher.
- **Lower rates for new entrants.** If you are under 26, or applying from a Student or Graduate visa, a lower rate can apply. You can be paid 70% of the going rate, with at least £33,400 a year. Your total stay on this rule cannot be more than 4 years, including time on a Graduate visa.

Before you enrol, find your target job on the [eligible occupations list](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skilled-worker-visa-eligible-occupations/skilled-worker-visa-eligible-occupations-and-codes). Then check its going rate in [Appendix Skilled Occupations](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-skilled-occupations).

## Who is a one-year UK master's a good fit for?

This is advice, based on the rules above. A one-year UK master's tends to suit you if:

- You know the job you want, and the course teaches for it.
- You already have some work experience and want to specialise or move up.
- Your target job is higher skilled on the UK list, and its usual pay meets the salary rules.
- You plan to go home, and employers there value a UK master's in your field.
- You want to spend as little time out of work as you can.

If you are not sure about the job yet, start with [how to choose a master's that leads to a job](/blog/choose-a-masters-that-leads-to-a-job/).

## Who should look at other options?

- **You want to bring a partner or children.** On most taught master's, you cannot. For postgraduate courses starting from 1 January 2024, this is limited to a PhD, another doctorate or a research-based higher degree. Government-sponsored students on a full-time course of 6 months or longer can also bring them. Read [can you bring family on a UK Student visa?](/blog/uk-student-visa-dependants/)
- **You need time to build experience.** One year of study and an 18-month Graduate visa is a short window. Look at a master's with a placement year, or at other countries.
- **Your target job is medium skilled or pays below the salary rules.** This matters if you want to stay in the UK long term.
- **Your budget is tight.** Compare the full cost with other countries before you decide.

To compare countries, read [which country should you study in?](/blog/which-country-should-you-study-in/)
