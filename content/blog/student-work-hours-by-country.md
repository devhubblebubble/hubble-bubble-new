As of October 2026, degree students can work 20 hours a week in term in the UK and Ireland. Canada allows 24 hours a week off campus, and Australia 48 hours a fortnight. Germany allows 140 full days or 280 half days a year. In South Korea, you need permission, and your Korean level sets your hours. All six allow more in the holidays, with conditions.

## How do work hours compare by country?

These limits are for full-time students, as of October 2026. Conditions are in the country sections.

| Country | During term | In holidays or breaks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | 20 hours a week at degree level, 10 below degree level | Full-time outside term time | Part-time students cannot work |
| Ireland | 20 hours a week | 40 hours a week | Stamp 2 only. Holidays: June to September, 15 December to 15 January |
| Germany | 140 full days or 280 half days a year, or 20 hours a week in the lecture period | No limit in the semester break | University assistant jobs do not count |
| Australia | 48 hours a fortnight | Unlimited in scheduled course breaks | No limit for research master's and doctoral students |
| Canada | 24 hours a week off campus | Unlimited in scheduled breaks | No limit on campus |
| South Korea | 10 to 35 hours a week, by course and Korean level | No limit for degree students with the Korean level, but check | Permission needed first |

## How many hours can you work in the UK?

The rule is paragraph ST 26.1 of Appendix Student to the Immigration Rules, last updated on 8 October 2026. Your limit depends on your course and your sponsor:

- Full-time study at degree level or above, with a higher education provider that has a track record of compliance: "20 hours per week during term-time", and full-time work outside term time.
- Full-time study below degree level, with such a provider: "10 hours per week during term-time", and full-time work outside term time.
- All other study, including all part-time study: "No employment permitted".

UKCISA explains that "'Term time' means the period when your Student sponsor expects you to be studying." Outside term time includes vacations and the periods before your course starts and after it ends. A week means "any seven-day period starting on a Monday".

See the [UK Student visa guide](/blog/uk-student-visa-guide/) for the other rules.

## How many hours can you work in Ireland?

Irish immigration says a student with Stamp 2 can work 20 hours a week in term time and "40 hours per week during vacation time". The vacations are June, July, August and September, and 15 December to 15 January. Its student permission page was last updated on 20 March 2026.

Before you start, you need an IRP card that shows Stamp 2, and a PPS number. Students with Stamp 2A cannot work. Citizens Information adds: "Your entitlement to take up employment ends when your permission to stay expires."

See [studying in Ireland](/blog/study-in-ireland/) for fees and Stamp 1G.

## How many hours can you work in Germany?

Make it in Germany says students from outside the EU can work "140 full days or 280 half-days per year". A working day of up to four hours counts as half a day.

You can instead work up to 20 hours a week during the lecture period. In the semester break, you can work without restrictions. The legal basis is section 16b(3) of the Residence Act, as of October 2026.

Student assistant jobs at a university do not count towards these limits, but DAAD says you must still tell the foreigners' office. Read more in [studying in Germany](/blog/study-in-germany/).

## How many hours can you work in Australia?

As of October 2026, Study Australia says the work limit on a Student visa is "a maximum of 48 hours per fortnight" while your course is in session. That includes study terms, semesters and exam periods.

You can work unlimited hours during scheduled course breaks, and after you complete your course while your visa is still valid. Master's by research and doctoral students have no limit once their course starts. You cannot work before your course starts, unless you hold another visa that allows it.

Check your own visa conditions in VEVO, the Visa Entitlement Verification Online system. See [studying in Australia](/blog/study-in-australia/) for costs.

## How many hours can you work in Canada?

IRCC says "You can work up to 24 hours per week" off campus during regular terms. The page was last modified on 15 April 2026.

During a break scheduled by your school, such as summer and winter holidays or a reading week, you can work unlimited hours off campus.

To work off campus, you must study full-time at a designated learning institution and have started your programme. It must last at least 6 months and lead to a degree, diploma or certificate. You also need a social insurance number. On campus, IRCC says "There is no limit to on-campus work hours" if you are eligible. Read more in [studying in Canada](/blog/study-in-canada/).

## How many hours can you work in South Korea?

You must first "obtain part-time employment permission from the local immigration office", and you need a set Korean level. Study in Korea's table gives these weekly limits for term time, as of October 2026:

- Korean language course, after 6 months, with TOPIK 2: 20 hours (25 with the higher limit)
- Associate degree, or bachelor's years 1 and 2, with TOPIK 3: 25 hours (30)
- Bachelor's years 3 and 4, with TOPIK 4: 25 hours (30)
- Master's or doctorate, with TOPIK 4: 30 hours (35)

The higher limit is for students at accredited universities or with excellent grades. Below the Korean level, the limit is 10 hours a week, or 15 for master's and doctoral students.

The table shows no limit at weekends and in vacations for degree students who meet the Korean level. Another line on the same page says the unlimited allowance does not always apply, so ask your university's international office before you rely on it.

The rules may change. On 7 October 2026, the Ministry of Justice announced plans to base permission on Korean ability and grades. These are plans, not rules yet. See [studying in South Korea](/blog/study-in-south-korea/).

## What about the UAE?

The UAE is not in the table. Its government portal says "Students aged 15 years and above are allowed to work and get trained at operating establishments", with a written contract, but gives no weekly hours limit. If you study at a [Dubai branch campus](/blog/branch-campuses-in-dubai/), ask your university what your visa allows before you take a job.

## What counts as work?

- **UK.** UKCISA says "Your pay and your working hours are separate." Unpaid employment "counts towards your weekly hours of work", and "'Voluntary work' is different to volunteering": volunteers "do not have a contract and must not be a substitute for an employee". A work placement "assessed as an integral part of the course" is allowed (ST 26.2).
- **UK, work you must not do.** Under ST 26.5, you must not be self-employed or engage in business activity, work as a professional sportsperson, sports coach or entertainer, or fill a permanent full-time vacancy. UKCISA says "'Permanent' means there is no end date in your contract."
- **Ireland.** Education in Ireland says Stamp 2 holders "must not engage in any other business or trade".
- **Germany.** Make it in Germany says any self-employment "requires the approval of the competent foreigners authority".
- **Canada.** IRCC says "Work is any activity that" you are paid for. Unpaid activity also counts if it is a job you would usually be paid for, or if it gives valuable work experience. Its example is an unpaid intern.
- **South Korea.** Study in Korea says allowed jobs are usually simple ones done by students, such as restaurant help, general office work, tourist guide assistance and duty-free shop sales.

## What happens if you break the rules?

UKCISA says "Work rights are a condition of your immigration permission." Official pages say this about breaking them:

- **UK.** The Immigration Rules say permission "may be cancelled where the person has failed to comply with the conditions of their permission" (paragraph SUI 11.8). A breach can also lead to a later application being refused (SUI 11.1 to 11.4).
- **Australia.** Study Australia says: "If you fail to comply with this visa condition, you may have your student visa cancelled."
- **Canada.** IRCC says: "Working more than 24 hours per week is a violation of your study permit conditions. You can lose your student status for doing this, and may not be approved for a study or work permit in the future. You may also have to leave the country."
- **Germany.** DAAD says you should know the labour laws for international students, "as you may be deported if you break them".
- **South Korea.** Study in Korea lists violating "the Immigration Act or other laws" among the grounds for cancelling a visa.

## What to do next

1. Check the work conditions on your own visa or permit before you accept a job.
2. Ask your university for its term and holiday dates in writing.
3. Keep a weekly record of your working hours, as UKCISA advises, and keep your payslips.
4. Plan your budget without part-time pay, since hours are limited and a job is not guaranteed.
