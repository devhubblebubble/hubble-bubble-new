Yes. As of October 2026, a UK Student visa accepts a student loan from a government or a government-sponsored student loan company. It also accepts an academic or educational loans scheme run by a regulated lender. The loan letter must be dated no more than 6 months before you apply. The 28-day bank balance rule does not apply to the loan.

## What does a loan have to cover?

A loan can cover all or part of the money you must show for the visa. That money is your unpaid first-year course fees plus a set amount for living costs, for up to 9 months.

As of October 2026, the living cost amount is £1,171 a month outside London and £1,529 a month in London. From 30 November 2026 it rises to £1,203 and £1,570. The full rules are in [Bank statements for a UK Student visa](/blog/uk-student-visa-bank-statements/), and a full budget is in [How much does it cost to study in the UK in 2026?](/blog/cost-of-studying-in-the-uk/).

## Which lenders count?

Appendix Finance (paragraph FIN 8.3) accepts a student loan provided by:

- a government
- a government-sponsored student loan company
- an academic or educational loans scheme from a financial institution regulated to issue student loans

For a UK lender, the regulator is the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) or the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA). For an overseas loan, it is the official regulatory body for issuing student loans in the country where the lender is and where the money is held.

So a loan from a bank or lender in India, the Gulf or Africa can count, if it meets these rules. It must be a student loan scheme, and the lender must be regulated for student loans in that country.

The rule says "financial institution", so it is not limited to banks. A non-bank lender can count too, if it is regulated to issue student loans.

## What must the loan letter say?

Under paragraph FIN 9.2, the letter from your lender must:

1. Be dated no more than 6 months before the date you apply.
2. Confirm it is a student loan from a government, a government-sponsored student loan company or an academic or educational loans scheme.
3. Confirm there are no conditions on releasing the money other than a successful application to study in the UK as a Student.
4. Confirm the amount of the loan.
5. Confirm the loan is to you, the applicant.
6. Confirm when the money will be available (see the next sections).
7. Confirm the lender meets the rule in FIN 8.3(c) on who can give the loan.

If the letter is not in English or Welsh, you also need a fully certified translation from a professional translator or translation company.

## How recent must the loan letter be?

The loan letter must be dated no more than 6 months before you apply. That is different from a bank statement, which must be dated within 31 days before you apply.

So a letter from early in your planning may be too old by the time you apply. Check the date before you book your visa appointment.

## Does the 28-day rule apply to a loan?

No. Appendix Student says you must hold the money for 28 days unless you rely on a student loan or official financial sponsorship that meets Appendix Finance. The Student caseworker guidance says the same.

The 28-day rule still applies to any money in a bank account that you use alongside the loan. Appendix Finance lets you combine a loan, official sponsorship and your own money. Your savings must then be held for 28 days in a row, with the latest statement dated within 31 days of your application.

## What if the money is paid after you arrive?

Check this part carefully. The letter must confirm the money will be either:

- available to you before you travel to the UK, or
- paid directly to your university before you travel, with any living cost part available to you by the time you arrive in the UK, or
- available before your course begins, if the loan comes from your own national government.

If your lender only releases money after you arrive, the letter cannot confirm the first two options. Unless the loan comes from your national government, it will not meet the rule. Ask the lender how and when the money is paid before you rely on it.

## What should you ask your lender for?

Whichever lender you use, ask for a letter that:

- comes from the lender itself and is dated within 6 months before your visa application
- confirms it is a student loan from an academic or educational loans scheme (or from a government or a government-sponsored student loan company)
- names you as the person the loan is to
- states the amount, and the currency
- says there are no conditions on release other than a successful Student visa application
- says when the money will be paid, and whether fees go to your university before you travel
- confirms the lender meets FIN 8.3(c), which means it is regulated to issue student loans

Before the lender writes the letter, finish the other loan paperwork. If conditions are still open, such as documents or collateral you have not yet given, the lender cannot confirm there are no other conditions.

The visa amount is in pounds. Money in another currency is converted at the OANDA spot rate on the date you apply, so leave a margin.

## What are the common mistakes?

- A loan letter dated more than 6 months before the visa application.
- A letter that lists conditions still to be met, other than the visa.
- A loan in a parent's name. The letter must confirm the loan is to you.
- Living cost money that is released only after you arrive.
- A lender that is not regulated to issue student loans in its country.
- A loan that covers only part of the total, with the rest not held for 28 days.

## What to do next

1. Work out the total you must show: the unpaid fees on your [CAS](/blog/what-is-a-cas/) plus up to 9 months of living costs.
2. Send your lender the list above, and ask how and when the money is paid out.
3. Time the letter so it is dated within 6 months of your visa application.
4. Hold any amount the loan does not cover in an accepted account for 28 days in a row.
