To study in the USA, you need an F-1 student visa, which starts with a Form I-20 from a SEVP-certified school. As of October 2026, you pay a US$350 SEVIS fee and a US$185 visa fee, and most applicants need an in-person interview. You can work up to 20 hours a week on campus in term, and OPT can give 12 months of work after your course.

## What kinds of universities and degrees are there?

EducationUSA says "The bachelor's degree usually takes four years to complete". After that, "The two main graduate degrees in the United States are the master's degree and the doctoral degree."

EducationUSA adds that "no official ranking system exists for colleges and universities in the United States". It advises you to start your search "at least 12 to 18 months prior to the academic year" you want to begin.

For an F-1 visa, your school must be approved by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP). If you may want the STEM OPT extension later, check accreditation too. The extension is for STEM degrees from "SEVP-certified and accredited U.S. colleges and universities".

The academic year is split differently at each university. Carnegie Mellon, for example, has a fall and a spring semester. Portland State's year is three quarters over nine months.

## How do you apply to a US university?

For a bachelor's degree, many colleges use the Common App, which lists more than 1,200 member colleges for first-year and transfer applicants. It announced the opening of its 2026/27 application season on 31 July 2026. If a college is not on the Common App, apply through its own website. The Common App says "Many colleges never charge an application fee, and many students qualify for fee waivers at those that do."

For a master's or PhD, you apply to each university separately. EducationUSA says "you are likely to have institutional and departmental application requirements". A graduate application usually includes a personal data form, a personal statement, your transcript and letters of recommendation. Check each programme's page for tests and deadlines.

Read [how to apply for a master's abroad](/blog/how-to-apply-for-a-masters-abroad/), [how to write a personal statement](/blog/masters-personal-statement/) and [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## How do you get an F-1 student visa?

1. **Get your Form I-20.** When a SEVP-approved school accepts you, it registers you in SEVIS and issues a Form I-20. You and a school official both sign it.
2. **Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee.** It is US$350 for F-1 students, as of October 2026. ICE says you can pay "in U.S. dollars by credit card on fmjfee.com, or by check, money order or Western Union". Spouses and children do not pay it.
3. **Complete the DS-160.** This is the online visa application. Upload your photo and print the confirmation page.
4. **Pay the visa application fee.** It is US$185 and non-refundable, as of October 2026. Some nationalities also pay an issuance fee (see below).
5. **Book your interview.** Since 15 July 2026, the State Department tells applicants to apply "in their country of nationality or residence". If you apply where you live, you "must be able to demonstrate residence" there.
6. **Attend the interview.** Bring a passport valid for at least six months beyond your stay, your DS-160 confirmation, your fee receipt and your signed I-20. You may also be asked for academic records, test scores, evidence that you will leave the USA, and how you will pay "all educational, living and travel costs".

Since 1 October 2025, the State Department says nonimmigrant visa applicants "will generally require an in-person interview with a consular officer", with a few exceptions that do not include students. A separate State Department page, updated on 18 September 2026, says its screening and vetting already applies to student visa applicants (F-1, M-1, F-2 and M-2).

For new students, a student visa can be issued up to 365 days before the course starts. You cannot enter the USA on it more than 30 days before the start date. You cannot travel to study on the Visa Waiver Program or a visitor visa.

## How much does it cost to study in the USA?

Costs vary widely. Here are three examples for 2026/27, from each university's page:

| University | What the figure covers | 2026/27 figure |
|---|---|---|
| Portland State University | Undergraduate at 12 credits a quarter: tuition, fees and living costs for 9 months | US$51,813 |
| MIT | Undergraduate cost of attendance, before aid | US$92,760 |
| Carnegie Mellon University | College of Engineering master's tuition for the year | US$61,510 |

- Portland State's total is US$25,632 tuition, US$5,775 fees (including health insurance) and US$20,406 living expenses.
- MIT's tuition is US$66,720. MIT says it is "need blind and full need for all of our undergraduate students, domestic and international".
- Carnegie Mellon estimates a further US$37,374 for housing, food, books, travel and other costs, on top of tuition.

## How much money do you need to show?

Each university sets its own cost estimate and checks your funds before it issues your I-20. Portland State, as one example, says it "cannot issue an I-20 without sufficient financial documentation". You must show funds for at least one year of study, and a visa officer may ask for proof for your entire degree.

You show more if family members come with you. For its nine-month year in 2026/27, Portland State adds US$10,698 for a spouse and US$8,243 for each child.

## Can you work while you study?

Yes, within limits. As of October 2026, Study in the States (run by the Department of Homeland Security) sets out these rules:

- **On campus.** "You may not work more than 20 hours per week when school is in session." You may apply for on-campus jobs up to 30 days before classes start.
- **Off campus.** This is "only available to F-1 students who have completed at least one full academic year", and you need permission first.
- **CPT (Curricular Practical Training).** Work that is "part of an established curriculum" and "integral to your program of study". Your designated school official (DSO) authorises it in SEVIS. It starts after one full academic year, unless your graduate programme requires it straight away. CPT is "not restricted by a weekly 20-hour work limit".
- **Pre-completion OPT (Optional Practical Training).** Limited to "20 hours per week while school is in session", and full-time during breaks.

If your DSO finds you working without permission, it must report this, and your SEVIS record will be terminated. You would then have to leave the USA immediately, and you may not be allowed to return. To compare countries, see [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay and work after you graduate?

You must leave the USA within 60 days after the programme end date on your I-20, unless you have permission to stay. A DHS rule published on 17 July 2026, with a start date of 15 September 2026, would cut this to 30 days. The State Department page still says 60 days, so ask your DSO which period applies to you. The main routes to work are:

- **Post-completion OPT.** "You can apply for 12 months of OPT at each education level." Your DSO recommends you, you file Form I-765 with USCIS, and you cannot start until USCIS issues your Employment Authorization Document. If you did one year or more of full-time CPT, you cannot get post-completion OPT.
- **The STEM OPT extension.** If your degree is on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List, you may apply for 24 more months. You "cannot be your own employer" for it.
- **The H-1B.** This status is based on a petition filed with USCIS. The State Department says a proclamation of 19 September 2025 restricts visa issuance and entry based on H-1B petitions filed after 12:01 a.m. EDT on 21 September 2025. The exception is a petition that comes with a payment of US$100,000. The Department of Homeland Security decides all exceptions, so ask your employer how this applies to you.

## Rules that depend on your nationality

Presidential Proclamation 10998, dated 16 December 2025, took effect on 1 January 2026. The State Department's page, last updated on 2 February 2026, says the Department "fully suspended visa issuance to nationals of 19 countries": Afghanistan, Burma, Burkina Faso, Chad, the Republic of the Congo, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Haiti, Iran, Laos, Libya, Mali, Niger, Sierra Leone, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria and Yemen. It also covers people using travel documents issued or endorsed by the Palestinian Authority.

For nationals of 19 more countries, B-1/B-2 visitor visas, F, M and J student and exchange visas, and immigrant visas are suspended: Angola, Antigua and Barbuda, Benin, Burundi, Côte d'Ivoire, Cuba, Dominica, Gabon, The Gambia, Malawi, Mauritania, Nigeria, Senegal, Tanzania, Togo, Tonga, Venezuela, Zambia and Zimbabwe. For Turkmenistan, only immigrant visas are suspended.

The same page says:

- The proclamation "only applies to foreign nationals who are outside the United States on the effective date" and who did not hold a valid visa on that date.
- "No visas issued before January 1, 2026, at 12:01 a.m. EST, have been or will be revoked pursuant to the Proclamation."
- Exceptions include dual nationals using a passport of a country not on the lists, and national interest exceptions decided case by case.

Your nationality also sets your visa's validity, its number of entries and any issuance fee, which you pay only after an approved interview. Find your country in the State Department's [reciprocity tables](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country.html), then choose F-1. Citizens of Canada and Bermuda generally do not need a visa to study, but they still need an I-20.

For your own country, see the Students from pages, for example [Afghanistan](/blog/students-from/afghanistan/), [Iran](/blog/students-from/iran/) and [Nigeria](/blog/students-from/nigeria/).

## What to do next

1. Shortlist courses at SEVP-certified schools, and check accreditation if you may want STEM OPT.
2. Check the proclamation lists and the reciprocity table for your nationality before you pay any fees.
3. Prepare proof of funds for the first year, and a plan for every year of the course.
4. When your I-20 arrives, pay the SEVIS fee, complete the DS-160 and book your interview.
