As of October 2026, a UK bachelor's degree usually takes three years, or four in Scotland, and a taught master's at least one year. You apply through UCAS for undergraduate study and directly to universities for a master's. The Student visa costs £558, plus £776 a year for healthcare. A Graduate visa then gives you 2 years if you apply by 31 December 2026, or 18 months after that.

## What kinds of universities are there in the UK?

The UK has universities and colleges that can award degrees, and each UK nation keeps its own official list. Before you apply, check that your degree will be a recognised UK degree. gov.uk explains how:

- In England, check the Office for Students (OfS) Register.
- In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, check that nation's list of recognised bodies.
- If you are not sure who awards your degree, ask your college or contact the awarding institution.

For a Student visa, you also need an offer from a licensed student sponsor.

## How long does a UK degree take?

These are typical full-time lengths, from the UK qualifications frameworks published by QAA:

| Qualification | England, Wales and Northern Ireland | Scotland |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's degree with honours | 3 years | 4 years |
| Integrated master's | At least 4 years | 5 years |
| Taught master's | At least 1 calendar year | At least 1 calendar year |
| Doctorate (PhD) | Usually 3 years | Usually 3 years |

A shorter course means fewer years of fees, but less time to build experience. Read [Is a one-year UK master's still worth it?](/blog/is-a-uk-masters-worth-it/) For Scotland's four-year degrees, read [what is different about studying in Scotland](/blog/study-in-scotland/).

## When do UK courses start?

Many courses start in September or October, and some start at other times of the year, UCAS says. For a master's starting in September or October, UCAS says to apply by June or July, and suggests around March to be safe.

Some universities also take students in January. The University of Birmingham is one example. For most taught master's starting in January 2027, its deadline for international applicants is 30 October 2026.

If you need funding from an organisation in your home country, UCAS says to allow at least four extra months.

## How do you apply to a UK university?

### Undergraduate courses: through UCAS

For 2027 entry, as of October 2026:

- The UCAS fee is £34.50 for up to five choices.
- You can send your application from 1 September 2026, once you have a reference.
- The deadline for Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses was 18:00 UK time on 15 October 2026.
- For most other courses, the deadline is 18:00 UK time on 13 January 2027.

UCAS says many universities keep accepting applications from international students until later in the year. Do not rely on it. Check each course page and apply early.

### Master's and PhD courses: directly to the university

For postgraduate courses, you apply directly to each university, UCAS says. A typical application needs a personal statement, two references and your official transcripts. A taught master's usually asks for a 2:1 or above.

Check whether your university charges an application fee. Birmingham, for example, charges nothing for taught postgraduate courses. Read [how to apply to a UK university without an agent](/blog/apply-to-uk-university-without-an-agent/) and [how to write a personal statement for a master's](/blog/masters-personal-statement/).

## How much does it cost to study in the UK?

Fees depend on the university, the course and your fee status. UCAS says international students usually pay more. At the University of Glasgow, for example, an international undergraduate pays £27,720 a year in arts and social sciences in 2026/27, or £33,210 in science and engineering. Students from the rest of the UK pay £9,790.

You also pay for the visa, and you must show money for your living costs:

| Cost, as of October 2026 | Amount |
|---|---|
| Student visa fee, from outside the UK | £558 |
| Health surcharge for students | £776 a year |
| Living costs to show, London | £1,529 a month |
| Living costs to show, elsewhere | £1,171 a month |

You show living costs for up to 9 months, plus your course fees for one academic year, which are on your CAS. For applications made from 30 November 2026, the monthly figures rise to £1,570 in London and £1,203 elsewhere. The money must be in your account for at least 28 days in a row.

For funding, see [government scholarships for international students](/blog/government-scholarships-for-international-students/).

## Which visa do you need to study in the UK?

For a full-time course at degree level or above, you apply for a Student visa. You must be 16 or over and have an offer from a licensed student sponsor. You also need enough money, and English at CEFR level B2 for degree-level study.

1. Get an offer. Your university then sends you a CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies), a reference number you need before you apply. See [what is a CAS](/blog/what-is-a-cas/).
2. Apply online within 6 months of receiving your CAS, and no earlier than 6 months before your course starts.
3. Prove your identity. From outside the UK, a decision usually takes 3 weeks.
4. Travel up to 1 month before your course, if it lasts more than 6 months.

On a degree-level course, you can usually stay for up to 5 years if you are 18 or over. If you hope to bring family, read [can you bring family on a UK Student visa](/blog/uk-student-visa-dependants/) first. The [UK Student visa guide](/blog/uk-student-visa-guide/) covers every step.

## Can you work while you study in the UK?

Yes, within limits. If your university has a track record of compliance, UKCISA says you can work:

- up to 20 hours a week in term time, on a full-time course at degree level or above
- up to 10 hours a week in term time, on a full-time course below degree level
- full-time outside term time

Part-time postgraduate students cannot work. On a Student visa, you also cannot be self-employed or work in certain jobs, such as professional sportsperson or sports coach. Compare countries in [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay and work after you graduate?

Yes, on a Graduate visa. You apply from inside the UK while you hold your Student visa, once your university has told the Home Office you completed your course.

| When you apply | Graduate visa length |
|---|---|
| On or before 31 December 2026 | 2 years |
| On or after 1 January 2027 | 18 months |
| After a PhD or other doctorate | 3 years |

The fee is £937, plus a health surcharge of usually £1,035 a year. You cannot extend a Graduate visa, but you may be able to switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa. Read [UK Graduate visa: 2 years or 18 months?](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-or-2-years/)

## Which rules depend on your nationality?

Some steps depend on your passport, or on where you have lived. As of October 2026:

- **TB test.** You need one if you are coming for 6 months or more, have lived in a listed country for 6 months or more, and lived there within the last 6 months. The list includes India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Kenya. It does not include the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Egypt or Turkey.
- **Visa brake.** Student visa applications made outside the UK are refused if the main applicant is a national of Afghanistan, Cameroon, Myanmar or Sudan. This applies to applications submitted online after 12:01am on 26 March 2026, even if you already have a CAS.
- **Lighter evidence list.** If you are a national of a listed country, you do not need to send proof of money, or the documents you used to get your offer, with your application. This applies only if you apply from the country where you live, or from inside the UK. UK Visas and Immigration can still ask for them. The list includes all EU countries, the USA, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait and Malaysia. India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Turkey are not on it.
- **ATAS.** Some master's and PhD courses need an ATAS certificate before you apply for the visa. Nationals of the EU, the EEA, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, Singapore, South Korea and the USA do not need one. It is free and takes at least 30 working days.
- **English.** Nationals of some countries, including Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand and the USA, do not need to prove their English.

## What to do next

1. Check that your university can award UK degrees and is a licensed student sponsor.
2. Note your deadline: 13 January 2027 for most UCAS courses, or each university's own date for a master's.
3. Check the rules for your nationality now, especially ATAS and the TB test, because both take time.
4. Budget for fees, the visa fee, the health surcharge and the money you must hold for 28 days.
