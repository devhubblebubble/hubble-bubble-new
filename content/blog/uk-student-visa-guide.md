As of October 2026, a UK Student visa costs £558 to apply from outside the UK. The health surcharge adds £776 a year. You must show your first year's fees plus £1,529 a month in London or £1,171 elsewhere, for up to 9 months. From 30 November 2026 these rise to £1,570 and £1,203. Apply up to 6 months before your course. Decisions usually take 3 weeks.

## Who can apply for a UK Student visa?

You can apply if you are 16 or over and have an unconditional offer from a licensed student sponsor. You also need enough money and a good enough level of English. If you are 16 or 17, you need your parents' consent.

Check that your university or college is on the [register of licensed student sponsors](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-students). Only a licensed sponsor can issue the CAS you need.

Full-time courses at degree level or above can lead to a Student visa. A part-time course only counts if it is above degree level, such as a master's. Full-time courses below degree level need at least 15 hours a week of organised daytime study.

Since 26 March 2026, the UK's [visa brake](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/visa-brake-changes-to-the-uk-visa-system) has applied to Student visa applications from four nationalities: Afghanistan, Cameroon, Myanmar and Sudan. If the main applicant is a national of one of them and applies from outside the UK, the application is refused, even with a CAS. Visas already granted are not cancelled, and GOV.UK says the brake will be reviewed regularly.

## What is a CAS, and why do you need it first?

A CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) is a reference number from your university. It confirms your place and records your course, your fees and how your English was assessed.

Universities issue it after your offer is unconditional and you meet their conditions. You need it before you can apply, and you must apply within 6 months of getting it. A CAS can only be used once.

Check every detail on it first. UKCISA warns that differences between your CAS and your application can lead to a refusal. [What is a CAS, and how long does it take?](/blog/what-is-a-cas/) covers the checks in full.

## How much does a UK Student visa cost?

As of October 2026, the application fee is £558 from outside the UK. It is also £558 to extend or switch from inside the UK. You pay the same fee for each family member who joins you.

You also pay the Immigration Health Surcharge as part of your application. For students it is £776 a year. If your visa is for more than a year and up to 18 months, you pay the yearly cost plus half.

Your visa length decides the surcharge. For a course of 12 months or longer, the visa usually starts up to 1 month before the course. It ends 4 months after the course. So a 12-month master's gives a visa of up to 17 months, and a surcharge of £1,164.

| Cost (as of October 2026) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Visa fee from outside the UK | £558 |
| Health surcharge, per year | £776 |
| Health surcharge, 12-month master's | £1,164 |
| Health surcharge, 2-year visa | £1,552 |
| Faster decision, where offered | £500 or £1,000 extra |

You may be able to pay for a faster decision. You are told if you can when you apply. Your English test, any TB test and your flights cost extra. For the full picture, see [How much does it cost to study in the UK in 2026?](/blog/cost-of-studying-in-the-uk/)

## How much money do you need to show?

You must show two things. The first is your course fees for one academic year (up to 9 months). The amount is on your CAS.

The second is your living costs. The amount depends on where you study and on the date you apply.

| Living costs | London | Outside London |
|---|---|---|
| Apply by 29 November 2026 | £1,529 a month | £1,171 a month |
| 9 months at that rate | £13,761 | £10,539 |
| Apply from 30 November 2026 | £1,570 a month | £1,203 a month |
| 9 months at that rate | £14,130 | £10,827 |

London means the City of London and the 32 London boroughs. The new figures come from a change to the Immigration Rules (HC 584). Applications made before 30 November 2026 are decided on the old figures.

If you apply in December for a January course, plan for the higher amount. Each partner or child who comes with you needs extra money on top. See [Can you bring family on a UK Student visa?](/blog/uk-student-visa-dependants/)

## Do all nationalities have to send bank statements?

No. Some nationalities may not need to send proof of money with the application. Their countries are on the gov.uk [differential evidence list](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money). As of October 2026, the list includes the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait, Malaysia, China and the EU countries. India, Pakistan, Nigeria and Saudi Arabia are not on it.

This only applies if you apply from the country where you live, or from inside the UK. If you live in one country and apply from another, send the evidence. The same rule covers the documents you used to get your offer, such as your certificates.

Even if you are on the list, UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) can ask for the evidence before it decides. Keep the money in place and your statements ready.

## What is the 28-day rule?

The money must be in the account for at least 28 days in a row. Count back from the closing balance on your latest statement. That statement must be no more than 31 days old on the day you apply.

You can use your own money or a parent's. A parent must give a letter agreeing that you can use it. Overdrafts, cryptocurrency, stocks and shares, and pensions do not count.

Money in another currency is converted at the OANDA rate on the day you apply. A student loan also counts, with a loan letter dated no more than 6 months before you apply. For the detail, read [Bank statements for a UK Student visa](/blog/uk-student-visa-bank-statements/) and [Can you use an education loan for a UK Student visa?](/blog/education-loan-uk-student-visa/)

## What level of English do you need?

For a course at degree level or above, your English must be equivalent to CEFR level B2. Below degree level, the level is B1.

For degree-level courses, your university can assess your English itself. It may ask you to take a different test from the approved Secure English Language Tests (SELTs). The level must still be equivalent to B2. Your CAS states how your English was assessed.

Some people do not need to prove their English. They include nationals of Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand and the USA. Check the [full list on gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/knowledge-of-english). To choose a test, read [Which English test do you need?](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/)

## Do you need a TB test?

You need a tuberculosis (TB) test if all of these are true:

- you are coming to the UK for 6 months or more
- you have lived in a listed country for 6 months or more
- you lived there, or in another listed country, within the last 6 months

So it depends on where you have lived. Your passport does not decide it. The [list of countries](https://www.gov.uk/tb-test-visa/countries-where-you-need-a-tb-test-to-enter-the-uk) includes India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya and Iraq. The UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman and Kuwait are not on it.

You must be tested at a clinic approved by the Home Office. If you are clear, the certificate is valid for 6 months from the date of your x-ray.

## Do you need an ATAS certificate?

You may need one for a master's or PhD in certain sensitive technology-related subjects. Some undergraduate courses with an integrated master's year need one too. Your university states on your CAS whether ATAS applies.

Nationals of EU and EEA countries, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, Singapore, South Korea and the USA do not need one. Check the [exempt list on gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/academic-technology-approval-scheme).

ATAS is free. It takes at least 30 working days (6 weeks), and can take longer from April to August. There is no fast-track service. You need the certificate before you apply for your Student visa, and it is valid for 6 months for a visa application.

## When can you apply, and how long does a decision take?

From outside the UK, you can apply up to 6 months before your course starts. You usually get a decision within 3 weeks. From inside the UK, you can apply up to 3 months before, and a decision usually takes 8 weeks.

You apply online. Then you prove your identity in one of two ways, depending on your passport. You use the UK Immigration: ID Check app, or you give your fingerprints and photo at a visa application centre. The centre could be in another country.

Put the steps in this order:

1. Meet your offer conditions and accept your unconditional offer. Your university then issues your CAS.
2. If you need ATAS, apply straight away. Allow at least 6 weeks.
3. If you need a TB test, book it at an approved clinic. The certificate lasts 6 months.
4. Put the money in your account and leave it there for at least 28 days.
5. Apply online up to 6 months before your course, and within 6 months of getting your CAS.
6. Prove your identity, then wait about 3 weeks for a decision.
7. Set up your UKVI account and link your passport to your eVisa.
8. Travel no earlier than your visa allows.

## What is an eVisa?

If your visa is approved, you get an eVisa. It is a digital record of your identity and immigration status. eVisas have replaced physical immigration documents. UKCISA notes that since 15 July 2025 you may not get a sticker (vignette) in your passport.

To see your eVisa, you need a UKVI account. Link it to the passport you will travel with. You can also get a share code to prove your status, for example to a landlord or an employer.

## How early can you travel to the UK?

If your course lasts more than 6 months, you can arrive up to 1 month before it starts. If it lasts 6 months or less, you can arrive up to 1 week before. You must not travel before the start date on your visa.

UKCISA also says not to travel if you cannot reach your university before its last date of enrolment.

## Can you work on a Student visa?

Yes, within limits. On a full-time course at degree level or above, you can work up to 20 hours a week in term time. This applies at a university with a track record of compliance. You can work full-time outside term time.

On a full-time course below degree level at such a university, the limit is 10 hours a week in term time. All other students, including all part-time students, cannot work.

No student can be self-employed or work as an entertainer. You also cannot work as a professional sportsperson or sports coach, or fill a permanent full-time vacancy. For more, read [How many hours can international students work?](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/)

After your course, you may be able to switch to a Graduate visa. See [UK Graduate visa: 2 years or 18 months?](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-or-2-years/)

## What are the common mistakes?

- Using the old money figures for an application made on or after 30 November 2026.
- Letting the balance drop below the amount during the 28 days, or using a statement more than 31 days old.
- Not checking the CAS. Differences between your CAS and your form can lead to a refusal.
- Applying more than 6 months after you got your CAS.
- Taking a TB test at a clinic that is not approved, or letting the certificate expire.
- Leaving ATAS too late. There is no fast-track service.
- Booking a flight for a date before your visa starts.

## What to do next

1. Check your university is on the sponsor register, then meet your conditions so it can issue your CAS.
2. Work out your money figure from the date you will apply, and keep it in the account for 28 days.
3. Check today whether you need a TB test or ATAS, because both take time.
4. Apply as soon as your CAS and documents are ready, up to 6 months before your course.
