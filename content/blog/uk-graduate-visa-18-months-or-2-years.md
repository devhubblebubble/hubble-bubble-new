It depends on the day you apply. As of October 2026, a Graduate visa lasts 2 years if you apply on or before 31 December 2026. It lasts 18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027. PhD and other doctoral graduates get 3 years either way. The fee is £937, plus a health surcharge of £1,035 a year.

## Who gets 2 years and who gets 18 months?

The length depends on the date you submit your application. It does not depend on when your course ends or when you graduate.

| When you apply | Graduate visa length |
|---|---|
| On or before 31 December 2026 | 2 years |
| On or after 1 January 2027 | 18 months |
| Any date, with a PhD or other doctoral qualification | 3 years |

The 18-month rule comes from a change to the Immigration Rules published on 14 October 2025 (HC 1333). The government said too many graduates were not moving into graduate-level jobs. PhD graduates keep 3 years.

## Why does your application date matter so much?

You cannot apply the day your course ends. You can only apply after your university has told the Home Office that you successfully completed your course. You must also be in the UK, and you must apply before your Student visa expires.

You do not need to wait for your graduation ceremony or your certificate.

Your Student visa usually lasts 4 months after the end of a course of 12 months or longer. After a course of at least 6 months but under 12 months, it lasts 2 months. That gap is your window to apply. If your course ends late in 2026, the window may run past 31 December 2026.

The examples below assume your university has reported your completion, and your Student visa is still valid on the day you apply.

| Course ends | You apply | Length you get |
|---|---|---|
| September 2026 | November 2026 | 2 years |
| September 2026 | January 2027 | 18 months |
| December 2026 | 20 December 2026 | 2 years |
| December 2026 | 5 January 2027 | 18 months |
| June 2027 | July 2027 | 18 months |

If your course ends in late 2026, ask your university when it will report your completion. If that is before 31 December 2026, apply as soon as it does.

## Who can apply for a Graduate visa?

You can apply if all of these are true:

- you are in the UK
- your current visa is a Student visa (or a Tier 4 (General) student visa)
- you studied a UK bachelor's degree, postgraduate degree or other eligible course on that visa
- you studied in the UK for the minimum time
- your university has told the Home Office you successfully completed the course

Other eligible courses include a PGCE, a PGDE, a law conversion course approved by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and the Legal Practice Course or Bar Practice Course in England and Wales.

Your university must be a licensed sponsor with a "track record of compliance". For a course longer than 12 months, you must have studied in the UK for at least 12 months. For a shorter course, you must have studied in the UK for all of it.

"Studied in the UK" means you were in the UK when your university needed you to be there. Lectures and meetings with tutors are examples.

## How much does a Graduate visa cost?

As of October 2026, the application fee is £937. You also pay the health surcharge, which is usually £1,035 for each year. This is the general rate, so it is higher than the student rate of £776.

For an 18-month visa, the surcharge rules say you pay one year plus half a year. That is £1,035 plus £517.50. You are told the exact amount when you apply.

| Visa length | Health surcharge | Total with the £937 fee |
|---|---|---|
| 18 months | £1,552.50 | £2,489.50 |
| 2 years | £2,070 | £3,007 |
| 3 years (PhD) | £3,105 | £4,042 |

The Immigration Rules for this visa set no money requirement, so you do not need to show savings. If your partner or children apply to stay with you, each one applies separately and pays the visa fee.

## What can you do on a Graduate visa?

You can:

- work in most jobs
- look for work
- be self-employed
- do voluntary work, if it is unpaid and for a registered charity, voluntary organisation or statutory body
- travel abroad and return to the UK
- keep living in the UK with your partner and children, if they are eligible

You cannot:

- apply for most benefits or the State Pension
- work as a professional sportsperson
- extend the visa

Your Graduate visa starts on the day your application is approved.

## How do you apply?

1. Wait for your university to report your completion to the Home Office.
2. Apply online from inside the UK, before your Student visa expires.
3. Prove your identity with the UK Immigration: ID Check app, or at a UKVCAS service point.
4. Stay in the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man until you get a decision. If you travel outside these places, your application is withdrawn.
5. Expect a decision within about 8 weeks. If you are approved, you get an eVisa.

## Can you switch to a Skilled Worker visa?

You cannot extend a Graduate visa. To stay longer, you may be able to switch to another visa, such as a Skilled Worker visa. That needs a confirmed job offer from a UK employer approved by the Home Office, and a certificate of sponsorship.

As of October 2026, the main rules for the job are:

- Skill level: since 22 July 2025, the threshold is RQF level 6, which is degree level. On the official list of occupations, these jobs are marked Higher Skilled. Medium skilled jobs only qualify in limited cases.
- Salary: at least £41,700 a year or the going rate for the job, whichever is higher.
- Lower rate for new entrants: if you are on a Graduate visa, you can be paid 70% of the going rate, as long as the salary is at least £33,400 a year.
- Time limit on that lower rate: your total stay in the UK on this basis cannot be more than 4 years, and that includes your time on the Graduate visa.
- English: level B2 if you are switching from another visa. A degree taught in English and awarded by a UK institution is one accepted way to prove it.

With 18 months, you have less time to find a job that meets these rules. Start your search during your course. ## Can your family stay with you?

Your partner and children can apply to stay if they were already your dependants on your Student visa. A child born in the UK during your stay can also apply. Other family members cannot join you as Graduate visa dependants.

If you later switch to a Skilled Worker visa, new dependants may be able to join you from outside the UK. For the rules while you study, read [Can you bring family on a UK Student visa?](/blog/uk-student-visa-dependants/)

## What to do next

1. Ask your university when it will report your completion to the Home Office.
2. If that is on or before 31 December 2026, apply as soon as it is reported.
3. Budget for the £937 fee and the health surcharge before you apply.
4. Start your job search during your course, using the Skilled Worker skill and salary rules as a guide.
