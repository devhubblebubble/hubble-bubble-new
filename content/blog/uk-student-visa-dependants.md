Only some students can. As of October 2026, you can bring a partner and children if you study a full-time PhD, doctorate or research-based higher degree of 9 months or longer. Government-sponsored students on a full-time course of at least 6 months can too. Most taught master's students cannot. Each dependant needs £845 a month in London or £680 elsewhere, for up to 9 months.

## Who can bring a partner or children?

For courses starting on or after 1 January 2024, two groups of students can bring dependants:

- a full-time student on a postgraduate course of 9 months or longer, where the university confirms it is a PhD, another doctorate or a research-based higher degree
- a student with a scholarship or sponsorship from a government for study in the UK, on a full-time course of at least 6 months

In the first group, the university must be a higher education provider with a track record of compliance. For courses that started before 1 January 2024, any full-time postgraduate course of 9 months or longer qualified. That older rule does not help new students.

| Your course | Can your family come as dependants? |
|---|---|
| Full-time PhD, doctorate or research-based higher degree, 9 months or longer | Yes, if the university confirms the course type |
| Any full-time course of 6 months or longer, with government sponsorship | Yes |
| Taught master's, without government sponsorship | No |
| Bachelor's degree, without government sponsorship | No |

There is one more rule, for families already in the UK. Your partner or child may already have dependant permission, or have had it in the last 3 months. If so, they may be able to apply again with you when you move to a new full-time course of 6 months or longer.

## What counts as government sponsorship?

The rules say the award must come from a government, for study in the UK. UKCISA notes that Home Office guidance currently looks for an award from your national government or the UK government.

The award does not need to cover all your fees and living costs. Your family must still meet the money rules. If you are not sure your award counts, ask your university's international team before you apply. To look for funding, see [Government scholarships for international students](/blog/government-scholarships-for-international-students/).

## Who counts as a dependant?

- your husband, wife, civil partner or unmarried partner
- your child under 18, including a child born in the UK during your stay

A child must not be married or in a civil partnership. They must live with you, unless they are in full-time education away from home. You will need evidence of the relationship, such as a marriage certificate or a birth certificate.

Parents, brothers and sisters cannot come as dependants. They can apply to visit.

## How much money do dependants need?

As of October 2026, each dependant needs £845 a month in London or £680 a month elsewhere, for up to 9 months. That is £7,605 or £6,120 per person. This is on top of the money you need for yourself.

The money must be held for at least 28 days in a row. The 28 days must end within 31 days of the application date. If your family applies with you, you show your own money plus their amounts. If they apply later, they only show their own amount.

The student's own living cost amounts rise for applications made from 30 November 2026 (HC 584). That change does not alter the dependant amounts. Here is an example for a student, a partner and one child applying from that date:

| Living costs for 9 months | London | Outside London |
|---|---|---|
| You (the student) | £14,130 | £10,827 |
| Your partner | £7,605 | £6,120 |
| One child | £7,605 | £6,120 |
| Total | £29,340 | £23,067 |

You must also show your first year's course fees. Each dependant also pays the £558 visa fee and the health surcharge of £776 a year. For the wider budget, see [How much does it cost to study in the UK in 2026?](/blog/cost-of-studying-in-the-uk/)

Your family may not need to show this money if they have been in the UK with a valid visa for at least 12 months. Nationals of countries on the gov.uk [differential evidence list](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money), applying at the same time as you, may not need to send the evidence. UK Visas and Immigration can still ask for it.

## What if your course does not allow dependants?

Then your partner and children cannot come as your dependants. This applies to taught master's and bachelor's students without government sponsorship.

Your options are:

- Choose a course that qualifies, such as a PhD or a research degree, if it fits the career you want.
- Look for a government scholarship that covers study in the UK.
- Your partner can apply for a visa in their own right, if they qualify for it on their own. One example is their own Student visa, with their own offer and CAS. Another is a Skilled Worker visa, with a job offer from a UK employer approved by the Home Office. They must meet every requirement of that visa themselves.
- Your family can visit. A Standard Visitor can usually stay for up to 6 months, and as of October 2026 the visa costs £135.

Visitors cannot work. They also cannot live in the UK for long periods through frequent or successive visits. Depending on their nationality, they may need a visitor visa, an electronic travel authorisation (ETA) or neither.

## Can your family join you after you graduate?

Not on a Graduate visa, unless they were already your dependants on your Student visa. A child born in the UK during your stay can also apply.

If you later switch to a visa such as Skilled Worker, new dependants may be able to join you from outside the UK. Skilled Workers' partners and children may be able to apply as dependants, but that visa has its own rules. See [UK Graduate visa: 2 years or 18 months?](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-or-2-years/)

## What about a child born in the UK?

A child born in the UK does not automatically become a British citizen. To travel in and out of the UK, your child needs their own dependant visa.

The rules have an exception for children born during your Student visa. UKCISA explains that a child can apply as a dependant in certain situations. One example is a child born during your current full-time Student permission of at least 6 months. Check UKCISA's family page for the conditions.

## What to do next

1. Ask your university whether your course counts as a PhD, doctorate or research-based higher degree for dependant visas.
2. If a government funds you, get the award letter and check that it names that government.
3. Add up the money for you and each dependant, and keep it in the account for 28 days.
4. If your family cannot come as dependants, plan visits or your partner's own application early.
