A study abroad consultant can be worth it if they help you choose the right career, course and shortlist. Make sure you know exactly what you are paying for. You do not need one to apply. As of October 2026, UCAS charges £34.50 for up to five undergraduate choices for 2027 entry. Some universities charge nothing for a master's application, and official advice services are free.

## What does a good study abroad adviser do?

A good adviser saves you time and helps you avoid expensive mistakes.

The British Council says a good agent should explain the UK education system, your options and visa requirements. They should help you prepare, gather visa documents, find suitable universities, apply, and compare offers and deadlines.

The most useful help often comes before any application:

- Career clarity: the work you want to do, and the course that leads there.
- A realistic shortlist: universities that fit your grades, budget and goals.
- Documents: a personal statement, CV and references that are clear and true.
- Timing: a plan for deadlines, English tests, offers and the visa.

The British Council also says an agent should help you make an informed decision. The final choice stays with you.

## Can an agent get you a better offer?

No. The British Council says agents cannot give you favourable entry requirements or other application options. They also cannot give you information that differs from what a student applying directly receives.

So the offer you get through an agent is the offer you could get on your own. What you pay for is advice, time and support.

## What can you do yourself for free?

Most of the application process is open to you directly. Here are the main free or low-cost options, as of October 2026.

| Task | Free or low-cost option |
|---|---|
| UK undergraduate application | UCAS, £34.50 for up to five choices (2027 entry) |
| UK master's application | The university's own website, free at some universities |
| Questions about a course | The university's admissions or international team |
| Visa and fee status questions | UKCISA's free student advice line |
| UK visa rules and costs | The Student visa pages on GOV.UK |
| Canadian study permit | IRCC says you can apply on your own |

Application fees for a master's vary by university. For example, Cardiff University says it does not charge an application fee for any postgraduate programme. The University of Oxford charges £75 for taught courses, as of October 2026. These are two examples, so check each university's own page.

UCAS lets you apply on your own. You fill in each section and add up to five choices. You also need a reference from someone who is not family, a friend or a partner. If you apply independently, UCAS says a school, college or UCAS registered centre can supply your reference.

UKCISA's student advice line gives free, independent and confidential one-to-one advice. It covers applying to university, applying for a student visa, your likely tuition fee status and funding. Its advisers are registered with the Immigration Advice Authority.

As of October 2026, the line is open on weekdays from 13:00 to 16:00 UK time, on +44 (0)20 7788 9214. Your phone provider may charge for the call. UKCISA does not give advice by email or social media.

GOV.UK has the official Student visa rules. As of October 2026, the visa costs £558 if you apply from outside the UK. You also pay the healthcare surcharge, which is £776 a year for students. For the full picture, read the [UK Student visa guide](/blog/uk-student-visa-guide/).

## When is paid help worth it?

This part is advice. Paid help makes most sense when the hard part is deciding what to do. It may be worth it if:

- you do not yet know which career or course you want
- you are choosing between countries and need an honest comparison
- you are changing field, or have a gap in your studies to explain
- you have several deadlines close together and little time
- your family wants a second opinion before paying a large deposit

If you want help after a visa refusal, use someone who is allowed to give immigration advice. For UK visa advice, GOV.UK says to check that an adviser is registered before you use them.

## When might you not need help?

You may manage well on your own if:

- you know your subject and the career you want
- you are happy to read university pages and follow each step
- you are applying to a few universities with clear entry requirements
- you have a school counsellor or a university contact who answers your questions

You can also do the research yourself and pay only for a final check. That can be a good middle way.

## What should paid help put in writing?

Ask for these in writing before you pay anything. They match what the UK National Code of Ethical Practice for education agents expects.

- every fee, and what each one is for
- which services the university pays for, and which you pay for
- the refund policy
- a signed agreement between you and the agent
- how to complain if something goes wrong
- which universities they formally represent, and which they do not

IRCC gives the same advice to students going to Canada. It says to get a written contract and a breakdown of the fees and the services you are paying for.

Never pay your tuition fee or deposit to an agent. The British Council says agents should not hold money or make payments on your behalf. Pay the university directly.

Be wary of promises. Governments make visa decisions, so no agent can promise you a visa. IRCC says no one can guarantee that a study permit will be issued.

## What to do next

1. Write down what you need help with: career, course, shortlist, documents or visa.
2. Try the free options first: university pages, UCAS, UKCISA and GOV.UK.
