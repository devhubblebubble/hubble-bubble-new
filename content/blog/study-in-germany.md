Unless you are from the EU or a short list of countries, you need a national visa for study, then a residence permit. As of October 2026, you must show €11,904 for the first year, usually in a blocked account. Most public universities charge no tuition, though Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students €1,500 a semester. After graduating, you can stay up to 18 months to find work.

## Who needs a visa to study in Germany?

Citizens of EU countries, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland need no visa or residence permit.

Citizens of Andorra, Australia, Brazil, Canada, El Salvador, Honduras, Israel, Japan, Monaco, New Zealand, San Marino, South Korea, the UK and the USA can enter without a visa to study. They must apply for a residence permit at the local foreigners authority within 90 days.

Everyone else applies for a national visa for study at a German embassy or consulate before travelling. As of October 2026, the visa fee is €75.

In Germany, you then get a residence permit for study. It is usually issued for two years at first and can be renewed.

Check your own embassy's checklist. For applicants in India, it includes a blocked account, the APS certificate and travel health insurance until you enrol.

## Do you need an APS certificate?

You need one if your school or university qualifications are from China, India or Vietnam. The Academic Evaluation Centre (APS) checks that your documents are genuine and good enough for study in Germany.

APS India says the certificate is generally required for the student visa. It depends on where your qualifications are from. Your nationality does not decide it.

Universities such as the Technical University of Munich (TUM) also ask you to upload it with your application.

APS India says checks take at least two weeks, and can take longer. Start as soon as you have your final documents.

From the summer semester 2027 intake, some Indian master's applicants must also take the dMAT, an academic aptitude test. It costs €150. As of October 2026, APS India says it will soon announce an updated list of the subjects affected.

## How much does it cost to study in Germany?

Most public universities charge no tuition fees. Freie Universität Berlin, for example, charges none except for continuing education programmes.

Watch for these exceptions if you are a non-EU student:

- Baden-Württemberg charges €1,500 a semester. It has done so since winter semester 2017/18, at universities such as the University of Stuttgart.
- The Technical University of Munich (TUM) has charged new non-EU students since winter semester 2024/25.

| TUM fee per semester | As of October 2026 | From winter 2027/28 |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's | €2,000 or €3,000 | €2,300 or €3,400 |
| Master's | €4,000 or €6,000 | €4,600 or €6,800 |

TUM has announced the higher rates for students who start from winter semester 2027/28. Students already enrolled keep the rate they started on.

### What is the semester contribution?

Every student pays a semester contribution, including at universities with no tuition. It pays for student services and, at many universities, a public transport ticket.

For winter semester 2026/27, it is €376.80 at Freie Universität Berlin and €402 at Universität Hamburg. Both include a €226.80 transport ticket.

## How much money do you need to show?

As of October 2026, you must show €11,904 for your first year. That is €992 a month.

Most students use a blocked account. You pay the money in before your visa. After you arrive, you cannot withdraw more than €992 a month.

You can also use a declaration of commitment from someone in Germany, or a scholarship.

The Federal Foreign Office says the amount is based on the maximum support rates for German students. The government plans to raise the main student grant, BAföG, from the summer semester 2027. Check the figure on your embassy's website before you pay in.

## Do you need health insurance in Germany?

Yes. You must prove health insurance that meets German standards when you enrol. Depending on your country, you may also need it for the visa.

The German missions in India ask for travel health insurance from your arrival until you enrol.

If you are under 30, statutory insurance costs about €120 to €130 a month at the student rate, says DAAD. DAAD gives no date for this figure, so check the current rate with the insurer. From age 30, you pay the standard contribution.

## Can you work while you study in Germany?

Yes. Students from outside the EU can work 140 full days or 280 half days a year. A half day is up to four hours.

You can instead work up to 20 hours a week during the lecture period. In the semester break, there is no limit.

Student assistant jobs at your university do not count towards these limits. Self-employment needs approval from the foreigners authority. To compare countries, read [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay in Germany after you graduate?

Yes. You can apply for a residence permit to look for qualified work. It lasts up to 18 months and cannot be renewed.

During those 18 months, you can take any job. Apply before your study permit runs out.

Once you find qualified work, you can apply for a skilled worker residence permit or an EU Blue Card.

## Do you need to speak German?

It depends on the language of your course.

- German-taught courses usually ask for the DSH or TestDaF German test.
- English-taught courses often ask for IELTS or TOEFL. In January 2026, DAAD counted about 2,400 programmes taught fully in English: 430 bachelor's and 1,950 master's.

Outside class, German matters. The Federal Employment Agency says good German improves your chances of finding a job, and that English alone is often not enough.

For the English side, see [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## How do you apply to a German university?

Many universities use uni-assist, which checks international applications for over 180 member universities. Check each course page to see whether you apply through it.

As of October 2026, uni-assist charges €75 for your first course choice each semester. Each extra choice costs €30.

## Is Germany a good fit for you?

Germany can suit you if low tuition matters most and you are ready to learn German.

Good points:

- Most public universities charge no tuition.
- You get up to 18 months to find a job after you graduate.
- There are about 1,950 master's programmes taught fully in English.

Downsides:

- You must put €11,904 in a blocked account before you get the visa.
- Many employers want German, even if you studied in English.
- The APS, uni-assist and the blocked account add steps, cost and waiting time.
- Baden-Württemberg and TUM charge non-EU students tuition.

To weigh Germany against other countries, read [which country should you study in](/blog/which-country-should-you-study-in/).

## What to do next

1. Shortlist courses, and note the teaching language and any tuition fee for each.
2. If your qualifications are from China, India or Vietnam, apply for the APS first.
3. Once you have an admission letter, open a blocked account for €11,904.
4. Book your visa appointment early and follow your embassy's checklist.
