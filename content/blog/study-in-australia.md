To study in Australia you need a Student visa (subclass 500). As of October 2026, it costs from AUD 2,500, you can work up to 48 hours a fortnight in term, and you may need to show AUD 29,710 a year for living costs. Since 2 October 2026, most students can no longer bring a partner or children. After graduating, the Temporary Graduate visa usually gives 2 to 3 years.

## What visa do you need to study in Australia?

You need the Student visa (subclass 500). It lets you study full-time at a recognised education institution and stay in Australia in line with your enrolment.

You usually need a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from your university before you apply. Your CoE must still be valid when Home Affairs decides your application.

## How much does the Student visa cost?

From 1 July 2026, the Student visa costs from AUD 2,500 per application, unless you are exempt. Study Australia says these prices are correct at July 2026.

Visa charges can change, so check the [Home Affairs Student visa page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500) on the day you apply.

## What is the Genuine Student requirement?

Every applicant must show they are a genuine student and that study is the main reason for the visa. This Genuine Student (GS) requirement applies to applications lodged on or after 23 March 2024.

You answer questions in the online form. Each answer can be up to 150 words, in English. The questions cover:

- your current situation, including ties to family, community, work and money
- why you chose this course and this provider, and what you know about living in Australia
- how the course will help you
- anything else relevant, plus an extra question if you have held a Student visa before

Home Affairs gives more weight to statements backed by evidence. Attach documents such as transcripts, details of your current job with a referee, and bank statements or tax returns. It also looks at your visa history, including any refusals.

A plan to apply for permanent residence later does not count against you. You still need to show that study is your main purpose.

## How much money do you need to show?

The living cost figure is AUD 29,710 a year for the main applicant. It has applied since 10 May 2024, and it is still the version in force as of October 2026.

The evidence you need is not the same for everyone. Study Australia points students to the Home Affairs Document Checklist Tool to see what to provide.

Treat the visa figure as a minimum. Study Australia warns that real living costs may be much higher than the amount needed for the visa.

## Do you need health insurance in Australia?

Yes. You must hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the whole time you study in Australia, unless an exemption applies.

The basic cover usually includes GP visits, some hospital treatment, ambulance services and limited medicines. It does not cover dental, optical or physiotherapy care. You can buy extra cover if you want it.

## How many hours can you work while studying?

You can work up to 48 hours a fortnight while your course is in session, including exam periods. During scheduled course breaks you can work unlimited hours.

If you study a master's by research or a doctorate, there is no limit once your course starts. You cannot work before your course starts. To compare countries, see [student work hours by country](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you bring your family?

For most students, no. From 2 October 2026, you cannot include a partner or children in your Student visa application unless an exemption applies. The exemptions are:

- PhD study
- sponsorship by DFAT or Defence
- a foreign government scholarship
- being an eligible student from a Pacific or ASEAN member country

Family members cannot be added after your visa is granted. Home Affairs says there are no exemptions to this rule. Applications lodged before 2 October 2026 are assessed under the rules in place when they were lodged.

The same changes stop most people on other temporary visas from applying for a Student visa inside Australia. They can still apply from outside Australia.

## Can you stay and work in Australia after graduating?

Yes, if you qualify for the Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485). The main conditions are:

- you are 35 or under when you apply (some exceptions apply)
- you have held a Student visa in the last 6 months
- you have a recent qualification from a CRICOS-registered course

Your qualification decides the stream:

| Stream | Who it is for | Usual length |
|---|---|---|
| Post-Higher Education Work | Bachelor's degree or higher | 2 to 3 years, by qualification |
| Post-Vocational Education Work | Diploma, associate degree or trade qualification | Up to 18 months |
| Second Post-Higher Education Work | Regional graduates already on a 485 | 1 to 2 years |

The 485 has become expensive. The charge for the main applicant rose from AUD 2,300 to AUD 4,600 on 1 March 2026, then to AUD 5,750 on 1 July 2026. Holders keep unlimited work rights. The government has also said it will restrict family members on the 485, with details still to come.

## Is there a cap on international students?

Not a hard cap. The National Planning Level is 295,000 new overseas student commencements in 2026, and it stays at 295,000 in 2027. The government says it is not a cap or a limit on student numbers.

It works through visa processing for applications lodged outside Australia. Visas are processed as a priority until a provider reaches its threshold, which is 80% of its allocation in 2026. After that, applications are still accepted but processed more slowly.

For 2027, public universities share 161,725 places and other higher education providers share 38,500. Some students are exempt, including higher degree by research students and school students. Ask your university how close it is to its allocation before you apply.

## Which English test do you need?

For tests taken on or after 7 August 2025, nine English tests are approved for Student visa applications: Cambridge C1 Advanced, CELPIP General, IELTS Academic, IELTS General Training, LANGUAGECERT Academic, MET, OET, PTE Academic and TOEFL iBT.

The level of English needed did not change in 2025, but some scores were adjusted to match it. For example, the TOEFL iBT score went from 64 to 67. Check the English score on your course page as well. For help choosing, see [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## Is Australia a good fit for you?

Australia can suit you if:

- you want a post-study work visa of 2 to 3 years after a degree
- you plan to work during term, up to 48 hours a fortnight
- you are coming alone, or you qualify for a family exemption

Think carefully if:

- you want to bring a partner or children, since most students no longer can
- your budget is tight: the visa is AUD 2,500, the 485 is AUD 5,750 and the living cost figure is AUD 29,710 a year
- your university is near its allocation, which can slow your visa

To compare options, see [which country should you study in](/blog/which-country-should-you-study-in/) or the guide to [studying in Canada](/blog/study-in-canada/).

## What to do next

1. Choose your course for the career you want, and check that it is CRICOS-registered.
2. Run the Home Affairs Document Checklist Tool to see what evidence you need.
3. Write your Genuine Student answers from your own plans, and attach evidence for each point.
4. Budget for the visa, OSHC and living costs, and for the 485 charge if you want to stay on.
