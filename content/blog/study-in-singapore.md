To study full-time in Singapore, you need a Student's Pass from the Immigration and Checkpoints Authority (ICA). As of October 2026, you apply online 2 to 3 months before your course, and pay S$45 to apply and S$60 for the pass. You can work up to 16 hours a week in term. The MOE Tuition Grant lowers your fees, but you must then work in Singapore for 3 years.

## What kinds of universities are there?

ICA's Student's Pass page for Institutes of Higher Learning lists:

- 6 local universities: the National University of Singapore (NUS), Nanyang Technological University (NTU), SMU, SIT, SUSS and SUTD
- 5 polytechnics: Nanyang, Ngee Ann, Republic, Singapore Polytechnic and Temasek
- offshore institutes with local campuses: ESSEC, INSEAD, SP Jain, DigiPen, TUM Asia and Assas International School

ICA says "To apply for a Student's Pass, you must be accepted into an approved full-time course in Singapore." Part-time courses, and courses run in the evening or at weekends, are not eligible.

## How do you apply to a university?

Apply to the university first. NUS, as one example, takes applications online, and its prospectus for 2026/27 entry gives an S$20 application fee. For English, it asks for IELTS 6.5 overall, with 6.5 in reading and writing, or another accepted test.

NUS says to check its website for the exact application dates. For help with each step, read [how to apply for a master's abroad](/blog/how-to-apply-for-a-masters-abroad/) and [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## How do you get a Student's Pass?

1. **Get your school's letter.** You need a Registration Acknowledgement Letter from the school you plan to attend.
2. **Apply online.** Use ICA's e-Service, SOLAR. ICA says to apply "at least two months and not more than three months before course begins". You give personal, education, employment and financial support details, your passport biodata page and a digital photo taken within the last three months. Documents not in English need official translations.
3. **Pay the processing fee.** It is S$45 and non-refundable, as of October 2026. ICA says processing is "within one week (and within two weeks if Visa is required)".
4. **Receive your IPA letter.** "If your application is successful, ICA will issue you an In-Principle Approval (IPA) letter." Show it at the immigration checkpoint. ICA warns that "the letter does not guarantee entry into or stay in Singapore."
5. **Complete formalities in Singapore.** Book an appointment and bring the documents listed in your IPA letter to the ICA Services Centre. Submit a Medical Examination Report if it applies to you. Pay the S$60 issuance fee, plus S$30 for a multiple-entry visa if that applies to you.

You do not need to be in Singapore while ICA processes your application. ICA recommends applying before you arrive, because you cannot extend your stay while you wait. Since 27 February 2023, the Student's Pass has been digital only.

## How much money do you need?

ICA's application asks for details of your financial support. Its Student's Pass pages do not set a fixed amount, so ask your university what evidence it expects.

For living costs, NUS estimates S$11,750 for 2026/27, including on-campus accommodation for 35 weeks.

## How much are fees, and what is the Tuition Grant?

The MOE Tuition Grant lowers your tuition fees. NTU's undergraduate fees for 2026/27 show the difference for most programmes. The figures include 9% GST.

| Student | Fee a year |
|---|---|
| International student from an ASEAN country, with the grant | S$18,050 |
| Other international student, with the grant | S$21,400 |
| Without the grant, lab-based programme | S$40,600 |
| Without the grant, non-lab-based programme | S$36,350 |

Business, accountancy, medicine and some other programmes have different fees, listed on NTU's page.

The grant comes with a bond. NTU says students who take it "are required to sign the Tuition Grant Agreement in which they will be contractually obliged to work in a Singapore entity for three (3) years upon graduation". MOE's page, updated on 23 June 2026, says "You must be employed full-time in a Singapore entity for 3 years."

If you do not take the grant, you pay the non-subsidised fee. Read the agreement in full before you accept.

## Can you work while you study?

Yes, within the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) rules. Its page, updated on 11 May 2026, says:

- **During term.** If you study full-time at an institution on MOM's list, such as NUS, NTU or SMU, you can work "for a maximum of 16 hours a week".
- **During vacations.** You can work if you are a full-time student at a listed institution, hold a Student's Pass and are aged 14 or over. The vacation list of institutions is longer.
- **Internships.** An industrial attachment or internship run by your university is allowed, but "it must contribute towards your graduation requirements".
- **Not allowed.** Exchange students cannot work. Working without a valid work pass is an offence.

To compare countries, see [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay and work after you graduate?

ICA says that "When you cease or terminate your studies, you should cancel your Student's Pass". It asks for this within seven days.

To work in Singapore, you need a work pass. MOM lists these two:

| Pass | Who it is for | Minimum salary now | New applications from 1 January 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employment Pass | Professionals, managers, executives and technicians | S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services) | S$6,000 a month (S$6,600 in financial services) |
| S Pass | Employees with a job offer who meet the salary for their age | S$3,300 a month at age 23 or below | S$3,600 a month at age 23 or below |

Both minimums rise with age from 23. For the Employment Pass, the current figure reaches S$10,700 at age 45 and above, or S$11,800 in financial services. The 2027 figures also apply to renewals of passes that expire from 1 January 2028.

Employment Pass candidates must also pass COMPASS, a points test. MOM says "Your application needs to earn 40 points to pass COMPASS." Candidates with a fixed monthly salary of at least S$22,500 are exempt.

If you took the Tuition Grant, plan your job search around the bond. Your 3 years must be full-time work in a Singapore entity.

## What are the common mistakes?

- Applying outside ICA's window, which is 2 to 3 months before your course begins.
- Choosing a part-time, evening or weekend course. These cannot lead to a Student's Pass.
- Accepting the Tuition Grant without reading the agreement and its 3-year bond.
- Working more than 16 hours a week in term, or working while on an exchange programme.
- Forgetting to cancel your Student's Pass within seven days when your studies end.

## Rules that depend on your nationality

- **Entry visa.** ICA's visa requirements page, updated on 25 June 2026, lists the countries whose citizens need a visa to enter Singapore. They include Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Egypt, India, Iran, Iraq, Nigeria and Pakistan, among others. Holders of some travel documents also need one, such as a Palestinian Authority passport or a temporary passport issued by the UAE.
- **The visa and your IPA letter.** ICA says "If you require a visa to enter Singapore, the visa will be automatically included in your IPA letter."
- **Fees.** Under the Tuition Grant, NTU charges international students from ASEAN countries less than other international students.

## What to do next

1. Check that your course is full-time, at an institution on ICA's list.
2. Decide whether to accept the Tuition Grant, and read the agreement first.
3. Apply for the Student's Pass 2 to 3 months before your course begins.
4. Check the current Employment Pass and S Pass salaries on MOM's site before you plan to stay.
