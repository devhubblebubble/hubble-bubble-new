Yes, a master's can help you change career, mainly through a conversion course built for graduates of other subjects. UK examples include computer science, psychology and law, though psychology and law need more training, exams or work experience afterwards. It works best when the new job values the qualification and you have tested the field first.

## What is a conversion master's?

A conversion master's is built for people whose first degree is in a different subject. It teaches the basics of a new field quickly, often in one year full time.

The UK government's careers website gives one example: "Graduates of other subjects can do a postgraduate master's conversion course to get into AI and data science." The site was called the National Careers Service until 1 October 2026.

Three common routes in the UK:

| Field | What the conversion gives you | What you still need |
|---|---|---|
| Computer science | A one-year MSc for non-computing graduates | Projects that show your skills |
| Psychology | BPS Graduate Basis for Chartered Membership | Further training to practise |
| Law (England and Wales) | Knowledge of the law (optional) | SQE1, SQE2 and work experience |

## How does a computer science conversion work?

The University of Bristol's [MSc Computer Science (Conversion)](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/study/postgraduate/taught/msc-computer-science-conversion/) is one example. It is for people moving into computing "from a non-computer science background". It assumes no prior knowledge of programming and lasts one year full time.

Bristol typically asks for an upper second-class honours degree, or an international equivalent, in a subject not related to computer science. Applicants with a degree in computer science or a closely related subject cannot be considered. As of October 2026, Bristol lists the overseas fee as £37,300 a year.

Check this rule on every course you look at. Some conversion courses are only for people without a related degree.

## How does a psychology conversion work?

A conversion course accredited by the British Psychological Society (BPS) is for graduates who have not taken an accredited psychology programme. The [BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/postgraduate-study-and-qualifications) says these courses usually last at least one full year, about 45 weeks full time. They typically carry 120 or 180 credits.

Passing one makes you eligible for the BPS Graduate Basis for Chartered Membership (GBC). GBC is the first step. It does not make you a practising psychologist.

To become a clinical psychologist, the [BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/clinical-psychologist-job-profile) describes three stages:

1. GBC, from an accredited degree or conversion course.
2. An accredited Doctorate in Clinical Psychology. Most applications go through a central clearing house.
3. Registration with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC).

The BPS says doctorate courses most want experience as an assistant psychologist or research assistant. It also warns that competition for assistant psychologist posts is "fierce". The doctorate at the University of Leeds, one example, lasts 36 months full time.

If you are an international student, check that doctorate places and funding are open to you before you pay for a conversion course.

## How do you change into law?

In England and Wales, you do not need a law degree to become a solicitor. The [Solicitors Regulation Authority](https://www.sra.org.uk/become-solicitor/sqe/) (SRA) says you need to:

1. Have a degree in any subject, or an equivalent level 6 qualification.
2. Pass both parts of the Solicitors Qualifying Examination: SQE1 and SQE2.
3. Complete two years of full-time, or equivalent, qualifying work experience.
4. Pass the SRA's character and suitability checks.

A law conversion course can teach you the law before the exams, but the SRA does not require one.

Qualifying work experience can be paid or volunteer work, in up to four organisations. You can gain it in England, Wales or overseas. A solicitor of England and Wales, or a compliance officer for legal practice, must confirm it.

The exams have their own cost. As of October 2026, for assessments from September 2026 onwards, SQE1 costs £2,006 and SQE2 costs £3,086.

These are assessment fees. Preparation courses are separate, and the SRA keeps a list of training providers.

## What do employers look for in a career changer?

This part is advice. Employers want to see that you can do the job and that you understand it. A new degree on its own may not show that.

Show them:

- **Proof of skills.** Projects, a portfolio, or a dissertation on a real problem in the field.
- **Proof of interest.** Short courses, volunteering, or conversations with people in the job before you applied.
- **Transferable skills.** The UK careers website gives teamwork, communication and problem solving as examples. Say where you used them in your old job.
- **A clear story.** Explain why this field, why now, and how your past work helps. Your personal statement is the first place to tell it. Read [how to write a personal statement for a master's](/blog/masters-personal-statement/).

## What are the risks?

- **Cost with no promise of a job.** You pay a full fee and a year of living costs. No course can promise you a job.
- **One year is short.** You start from the basics and still need experience. In the UK, the Graduate visa lasts 18 months if you apply on or after 1 January 2027. Read [UK Graduate visa: 2 years or 18 months?](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-or-2-years/)
- **The master's may be only the first step.** Clinical psychology needs a doctorate. Law needs the SQE and two years of work experience.
- **Starting again.** Your first job in the new field may pay less than your current one. If you need a UK work visa, check the salary rules in [is a one-year UK master's still worth it?](/blog/is-a-uk-masters-worth-it/)
- **Entry rules.** Some conversion courses exclude people with a related degree. Some next stages may not be open to international applicants.

## How can you test a new field before you pay?

Spend a month or two testing the field. It costs little, and it can save you a year.

1. **Take a short online course.** Bristol encourages applicants to try "a basic online course in programming (in any language)" before applying, "so they can confirm they have the appetite and aptitude for this subject area".
2. **Talk to three people who do the job.** Ask about a normal week, the hardest part, and how they got in.
3. **Do a small project.** Build a simple app, analyse a public dataset, or write a short case study in the field.
4. **Volunteer or shadow someone.** The UK careers website notes that volunteering can build skills that transfer to a future role.
5. **Read ten job adverts.** List the skills they ask for, and check how many the course would give you.

## What to do next

1. Pick one target job and read its official job profile.
2. Test the field for a month or two with a short course, a project and some conversations.
3. Write down the full route: course, exams, training and work experience, with the cost of each.
4. Shortlist conversion courses and check who they accept.
