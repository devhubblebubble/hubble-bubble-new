A CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) is a reference number from a licensed student sponsor, such as a UK university. It confirms your place, and you need it to apply for a Student visa. Universities issue it after your offer is unconditional and you meet their conditions, often a deposit and some checks. There is no fixed time. Once you have it, you must apply within 6 months.

## What is a CAS?

A CAS is an electronic record, not a paper certificate. Your university creates it in a Home Office system and sends you its unique reference number. You enter that number on your Student visa application.

Each CAS is for one course at one university. If you received more than one, UKCISA says the others become obsolete once you choose where to study. You must study at the university that issued the CAS you use.

You will usually also get a CAS statement, which shows what the CAS holds. UKCISA says you do not need to submit the statement with your application, but keep it, because it is useful.

## What does a CAS contain?

The Immigration Rules list what your university must put on it. This includes:

- the course title, academic level, and start and end dates
- the hours of study each week, and whether the course is full-time or part-time
- the address of your main place of study
- the cost of your fees and any university accommodation, and how much you have paid
- how the university assessed your English
- whether you need an ATAS certificate
- details of any work placement on the course
- if you studied in the UK before, confirmation that the new course meets the academic progress rules

It also lists the qualifications the university used to make your offer, under "Evidence used to obtain offer". The University of East Anglia, as one example, says its CAS also shows your passport number and the university's sponsor licence number.

## When do universities issue a CAS?

Universities issue a CAS after they make you an unconditional offer. Before they do, the Home Office expects them to be satisfied that you will meet the visa rules. So universities often ask you to complete some steps first:

1. Meet your offer conditions and accept your unconditional offer.
2. Pay a deposit, if the university asks for one.
3. Upload documents, such as your passport, transcripts and English test result.
4. Show how you will pay your fees and living costs.
5. Attend a pre-CAS interview, if you are invited.

Here are two named examples. The University of Westminster says new students "must have accepted their unconditional offer and paid a deposit before we can issue a CAS". UEA asks you to accept your offer, pay a deposit, upload documents and show your finances. It says you may be asked to do a pre-CAS interview.

If your offer is still conditional, start with [Conditional or unconditional offer: what to do next](/blog/conditional-vs-unconditional-offer/).

## How long does it take to get a CAS?

There is no set time. It depends on your university and on how quickly you complete its steps.

As one example, UEA says it "will start to issue CAS from June onwards to students with unconditional offers who have paid their deposit". It also says it cannot say exactly how long a CAS will take, because this depends on many factors.

You can speed things up on your side. Pay the deposit, send every document the university asks for in one go, and reply quickly to any questions.

Plan backwards from your course start date. You can apply for your visa up to 6 months before the course starts. A decision from outside the UK usually takes 3 weeks. If your course needs ATAS, that takes at least 30 working days, so apply for it as early as you can.

## How long is a CAS valid?

You must apply for your visa within 6 months of receiving your CAS. The Home Office guidance for universities says a CAS is valid for 6 months from the date it is assigned.

A CAS can only be used once. If your visa is refused and you want to apply again, you need a new CAS. If your application was withdrawn or rejected as invalid, the same CAS can be used again.

If you change course or university, you normally need a new CAS. The University of Westminster, for example, says a CAS "will expire six months after it is issued if it is not used". If yours expires before you apply, ask for a new one.

## What should you check on your CAS before you apply?

Check it the day it arrives. Look at:

- your name, other personal details and passport number, using the passport you will apply with
- the course title, level, and start and end dates
- the fees, and the amount it says you have paid
- any accommodation fees paid to the university
- how your English was assessed
- whether it says you need ATAS
- the qualifications listed under "Evidence used to obtain offer"

If anything is wrong, ask the university to correct it before you apply. UKCISA warns that differences between your CAS and your application form can lead to a refusal.

The fee lines matter for your money. The amount you must show for fees is on your CAS. If a payment you made is not on it, UKCISA says you need a receipt for it. See [Bank statements for a UK Student visa](/blog/uk-student-visa-bank-statements/).

Keep the certificates for the qualifications listed on your CAS ready. UKCISA says you will not be required to submit them in some cases. One is when you study at degree level or above with a higher education provider that has a track record of compliance. Have them to hand anyway.

## Is a CAS the same as an offer or a visa?

No. They are three different documents, from different places.

| Document | Who gives it | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Offer letter | Your university | You have a place, perhaps with conditions |
| CAS | Your university, as a licensed sponsor | Your place is confirmed for your visa application |
| eVisa | UK Visas and Immigration | You have permission to come to the UK |

A CAS does not mean your visa is approved. You still need to meet every visa rule, including the money, English and any TB test or ATAS. The [UK Student visa guide](/blog/uk-student-visa-guide/) covers each step.

## What to do next

1. Meet your offer conditions, accept your unconditional offer and pay any deposit.
2. Send every document your university asks for, and attend any pre-CAS interview.
3. Check your CAS the day it arrives, and get any error fixed before you apply.
4. Apply for your visa within 6 months of receiving your CAS, and no more than 6 months before your course starts.
