To study a degree in Ireland from outside the EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland, you need Stamp 2 permission and an Irish Residence Permit. Many students also need a 'D' study visa first. As of October 2026, you must show €10,000 a year on top of fees, pay a €300 registration fee and hold private medical insurance. After a master's, you can stay up to two years to work.

## Who needs a visa to study in Ireland?

It depends on your nationality. The Irish government's list of visa-required nationalities includes India, Nigeria, Pakistan and Egypt. Citizens of the UAE, Brazil and Malaysia do not need a visa, but they still need permission to enter when they arrive.

If you need a visa and your course is longer than three months, you apply for a long-term 'D' study visa. As of October 2026, it costs €60 for one entry or €100 for multiple entries.

These are the main steps:

1. Get a place on a full-time course with at least 15 hours of study a week.
2. Pay your fees. If they are under €6,000, pay them in full before you apply. If they are more, pay at least €6,000.
3. Apply online up to 3 months before you travel. Expect a decision in about 8 weeks.
4. After you arrive, register with immigration if you are staying more than 90 days. You get an Irish Residence Permit (IRP) card.

The registration fee is €300 per person, paid by credit or debit card. You renew your permission each year.

Students on a course listed on the ILEP get Stamp 2. Students on other courses get Stamp 2A, which does not allow any work. Ask your university to confirm that your course is on the list.

## How much are tuition fees in Ireland?

Fees depend on the university and the course. These examples come from two universities' own fee pages, as of October 2026.

| University | Course | Year | Non-EU fee a year |
|---|---|---|---|
| University of Limerick | MSc Business Analytics | 2026/27 | €20,400 |
| University of Limerick | MSc Software Engineering | 2026/27 | €20,800 |
| University College Cork | MSc Business Analytics | 2027/28 | €23,314 |
| University College Cork | MSc Computing Science | 2027/28 | €29,614 |
| University College Cork | BComm (Hons), 4 years | 2027/28 | €22,814 |

Limerick's fees include a €104 student levy. Cork's fees include a €214 capitation fee. Cork's postgraduate fee page also says tuition can rise for each later year of a programme.

These are examples only. Always check the fee page for your own course and start year.

## How much money do you need to show?

As of October 2026, you must show €10,000 for each year of study. This is on top of your course fees.

- If you need a visa, you show the money with your visa application. You need immediate access to at least €10,000, and access to the same amount for each later year.
- If you do not need a visa, you show it when you register. You need direct access on arrival to €10,000 for a course of more than 8 months.
- For a course of 8 months or less, the figure is €833 a month, or €6,665 in total.

A sponsor, such as a parent, can support you. You must name each sponsor, prove how you are related and show their finances.

Degree students can also use a pilot scheme. You lodge an education bond of at least €10,000 with an approved student fees payment service. The receipt can replace bank statements.

Plan for more than the minimum. University College Cork estimates living costs of €1,240 to €1,880 a month, including accommodation.

## Do you need health insurance to study in Ireland?

Yes. You need private medical insurance for the visa application.

When you first register, private medical insurance or travel insurance with medical cover is accepted. It must cover accidents and hospital stays in Ireland. When you renew, travel insurance is not enough.

## Can you work while you study in Ireland?

Yes, with Stamp 2. You can work up to 20 hours a week in term time. In the holidays, you can work up to 40 hours a week.

The holiday periods are June to September inclusive, and 15 December to 15 January.

You need your IRP card and a PPS number before you start work. You cannot be self-employed. Stamp 2A holders cannot work at all.

Do not plan to pay your fees from part-time work. The visa rules say you must be able to support yourself without relying on casual work. To compare countries, read [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay and work in Ireland after you graduate?

Yes, through the Third Level Graduate Programme. It gives you Stamp 1G permission, and you can work up to 40 hours a week.

| Your Irish award | NFQ level | Stamp 1G |
|---|---|---|
| Honours bachelor's or higher diploma | Level 8 | 12 months |
| Master's or postgraduate diploma | Level 9 | 12 months, then 12 more |
| Doctoral degree | Level 10 | 12 months, then 12 more |

For Level 9 and above, you get the second 12 months if you show you have taken steps to find graduate-level work.

There is also an overall limit on your total time on Stamp 2 and Stamp 1G. It is seven years for Level 8 graduates and eight years for Level 9 and above.

## What language will you study in?

You will study and live in English. Education in Ireland describes Ireland as the only English-speaking country in the Eurozone.

For the visa, you send a certificate from your English test provider. Education in Ireland says it must be dated within two years of your course start date. Each university sets its own scores, so check the course page. See [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## Is Ireland a good fit for you?

Ireland can suit you if you want to study in English inside the Eurozone, with a clear route to work after a master's. Weigh the costs first.

Good points:

- You study and live in English.
- A master's can give you up to two years on Stamp 1G.
- You can work up to 40 hours a week in the summer and over Christmas.

Downsides:

- Living costs can be well above the €10,000 you must show. Cork's estimate comes to €14,880 to €22,560 over 12 months.
- After an honours bachelor's, Stamp 1G lasts only 12 months.
- The second year of Stamp 1G after a master's is not automatic.

## What to do next

1. Check whether your nationality needs a visa on the Irish immigration website.
2. Shortlist two or three courses, and ask each university to confirm the course gives Stamp 2.
3. Prepare your fee payment, your proof of €10,000 and your private medical insurance.
4. Apply online up to 3 months before your travel date.
