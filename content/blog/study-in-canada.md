To study in Canada you need a study permit. As of October 2026, it costs CAD 150, plus CAD 85 for biometrics, and a single student outside Quebec must show CAD 23,448 a year for living costs on top of tuition and travel. Most students also need a provincial attestation letter, but master's and doctoral students at public institutions have been exempt since 1 January 2026.

## What do you need for a Canadian study permit?

To get a study permit, you must:

- be enrolled at a designated learning institution (DLI)
- prove you have enough money for tuition, living costs and return travel
- prove to an officer that you will leave Canada when your permit expires
- get a medical exam and a police certificate, if required

Most students also need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL or TAL). If you will study in Quebec, you generally need a Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ).

## How much does a study permit cost?

As of October 2026, the IRCC fee list shows CAD 150 for a study permit and CAD 85 per person for biometrics.

Processing times vary by country. Check the IRCC processing times tool for your country before you plan your dates.

## What is a PAL, and who is exempt?

A PAL or TAL is a letter from the province or territory where you plan to study. It confirms there is space for you. Your DLI provides it.

You do not need one if, for example, you are:

- in a degree-granting master's or doctoral programme at a public DLI (from 1 January 2026)
- a primary or secondary school student
- an exchange student who pays no tuition to the Canadian DLI
- holding a scholarship from Global Affairs Canada

If you do not send a PAL or TAL, you must send proof that you are exempt.

## How does the study permit cap work in 2026?

IRCC expects to issue up to 408,000 study permits in 2026. That is 155,000 for new arrivals and 253,000 extensions for current and returning students.

For applicants who need a PAL or TAL, 309,670 application spaces are available, and up to 180,000 permits are expected to be issued. The 2026 target is 7% lower than the 2025 target of 437,000.

Master's and doctoral students at public DLIs are exempt from 1 January 2026, with a separate target of 49,000. IRCC published these figures on 25 November 2025. That notice gives no study permit figure for 2027.

## How much money do you need to show?

For applications made on or after 1 September 2026, a single student outside Quebec must show CAD 23,448 a year for living costs. This does not include tuition or travel, which you must also show you can pay.

The figure was CAD 22,895 from 1 January 2025 to 31 August 2026. IRCC updates the amounts each year. The total rises with each family member, for example to CAD 35,888 for 3 people. Quebec sets its own amounts.

You can show the money with, for example:

- bank statements for the past 6 months
- a guaranteed investment certificate (GIC) from a Canadian financial institution
- a student loan or a scholarship
- a receipt for first-year tuition and housing
- a letter from a sponsor, with proof of their income

## Do you need health insurance in Canada?

Health cover depends on the province or territory where you study. Some give international students free public cover, sometimes after a period of living there. In Ontario, all international students must have cover through the University Health Insurance Plan.

Where there is no free cover, your university or college will ask you to buy private insurance. Some plans cover prescription drugs and some do not, so ask your school how it works before you arrive.

## How many hours can you work while studying?

You can work up to 24 hours a week off campus during academic sessions. During scheduled breaks, such as summer and winter holidays or reading week, you can work unlimited hours.

To work off campus, you must be a full-time student at a DLI, in a programme of at least 6 months that leads to a degree, diploma or certificate. You also need a social insurance number (SIN). To compare countries, see [student work hours by country](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay and work in Canada after graduating?

Yes, with a post-graduation work permit (PGWP), if you meet the rules:

- your programme was at least 8 months long, at a PGWP-eligible DLI
- you studied full-time in every semester (your final semester can be part-time)
- you apply within 180 days of being told you have completed your programme
- you meet the language level for your programme, in all four skills

| Your programme | Language level | Field of study rule | PGWP length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Master's, 8 months or longer | CLB 7 or NCLC 7 | No | 3 years |
| Bachelor's or doctorate | CLB 7 or NCLC 7 | No | Programme length, or 3 years if 2 years or longer |
| College or other non-university programme | CLB 5 or NCLC 5 | Yes | Programme length, or 3 years if 2 years or longer |

The field of study rule applies if you applied for your study permit on or after 1 November 2024. You must then graduate in a field linked to long-term job shortages. Graduates of other university programmes also need CLB 7 and must meet this rule.

Your PGWP cannot last longer than your passport, so renew your passport first if it expires soon.

## Can your partner work in Canada while you study?

Only in some cases. Since 21 January 2025, your spouse or common-law partner may get an open work permit if you are studying:

- a master's degree programme of 16 months or longer
- a doctoral degree programme
- certain professional degrees, such as medicine, law, engineering, nursing or education
- on a listed programme, such as the Francophone Minority Communities Student Pilot

The permit usually lasts as long as your study permit.

## What English or French level do you need?

For admission, check the English or French level on your course page. For the PGWP, you need a test result at CLB 7 for a degree and CLB 5 for a college programme. Plan this early if you want to stay and work. For help choosing, see [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## Is Canada a good fit for you?

Canada can suit you if:

- you want a master's, since graduate students at public DLIs need no PAL and can get a 3-year PGWP
- you want to work during your studies, up to 24 hours a week off campus
- you can reach CLB 7 in English or French by the time you graduate

Think carefully if:

- you plan a college diploma, because the cap and the field of study rule apply
- you want your partner to work, unless your programme is on the eligible list
- your savings only just cover CAD 23,448 plus tuition and travel

To compare options, see [which country should you study in](/blog/which-country-should-you-study-in/) or the guide to [studying in Australia](/blog/study-in-australia/).

## What to do next

1. Check that your programme is at a DLI and is eligible for a PGWP.
2. Ask your DLI whether you need a PAL or TAL, or which exemption applies to you.
3. Prepare 6 months of bank statements, or a GIC, that cover CAD 23,448 plus tuition and travel.
4. Book an English or French test early if you plan to apply for a PGWP.
