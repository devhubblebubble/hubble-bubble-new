There is no single EU student visa. Each country issues its own long-stay visa and residence permit. Outside Ireland and Denmark, EU law sets two minimums: at least 15 hours of work a week, and at least nine months after graduating to look for work. As of October 2026, most German public universities charge no tuition, while France charges non-EU students €2,902 a year for a bachelor's.

## What is the same in every EU country?

The Students and Researchers Directive, (EU) 2016/801, sets minimum rules for students from outside the EU. Countries had to bring it into their own law by 23 May 2018. The figures below are minimums, so a country can give you more.

- **Work.** You can work outside your study time. Each country sets the limit. It "shall not be less than 15 hours per week", or the same in days or months a year (Article 24). Some countries ask for a work permit first.
- **Job search.** After you graduate, you can stay in the country where you studied for at least nine months, to look for work or set up a business (Article 25). The country can set a minimum degree level, no higher than level 7 of the European Qualifications Framework. After at least three months, it can ask for proof that you have a genuine chance of a job or of starting a business.
- **Study in a second country.** You can study part of your course in another EU country for up to 360 days in each country (Article 31). This applies if you are on an EU or multilateral programme, or under an agreement between universities.
- **Getting a permit.** You need health insurance and enough money to live on (Article 7). The permit lasts at least one year, or the length of your course if that is shorter (Article 18).

Ireland and Denmark are not bound by the directive (recitals 65 and 66). For Ireland's own rules, see [studying in Ireland](/blog/study-in/ireland/).

## Do you need a visa to study in Europe?

If you are from outside the EU, you will usually need a long-stay visa, then a residence permit. Each country issues its own, with its own fees and money rules. Four examples, as of October 2026:

| Country | Visa or permit | Money to show |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | Study visa | €11,904 a year |
| France | VLS-TS student visa, validated within 3 months of arrival, with a €100 tax | €877.50 a month |
| Italy | Type D study visa, then a residence permit within 8 working days of arrival | €10,179.85 a year |
| Sweden | Residence permit, SEK 1,500 fee | SEK 10,656 a month, for 2026 applications |

In Sweden, you must pay any tuition fees before you apply for the permit. For Germany's steps, see [studying in Germany](/blog/study-in-germany/).

## Can you travel around Europe with a student permit?

Yes, for short visits in the Schengen area. Schengen has 29 countries: 25 EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Bulgaria and Romania joined on 1 January 2025.

- With a valid residence permit or long-stay visa from a Schengen country, you do not need a visa to visit the others.
- Short stays are limited to 90 days in any 180-day period.
- Ireland has an opt-out and sets its own visa rules.
- Cyprus is not yet fully in Schengen, and its residence permits are not valid for travel in the Schengen area.

To study part of your course in another EU country, use the mobility rule above.

## How much is tuition for non-EU students?

At public universities, as of October 2026:

| Country | Tuition for non-EU students | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | None at most public universities | Baden-Württemberg: €1,500 a semester |
| France | €2,902 a year for a bachelor's, €3,950 for a master's (2026/27) | EU students pay €178 and €255 |
| Netherlands | Average €9,000 to €20,000 for a bachelor's, €12,000 to €30,000 for a master's | University of Amsterdam master's, 2027/28: €13,200 to €37,200 a year |
| Italy | Check each university | Politecnico di Milano: up to €3,943.04 a year |
| Spain | Check each university | Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya (UPC) master's: €45 a credit |
| Sweden | Each university publishes its own fees | Uppsala, MSc Computer Science: SEK 300,000 in total |

What the examples mean:

- **Germany:** every student also pays a semester contribution. See [studying in Germany](/blog/study-in-germany/).
- **France:** universities may waive fees on request, based on your circumstances. A decree of 19 May 2026 changed the waiver rules, so ask what your university offers.
- **Italy:** at Politecnico di Milano and the University of Bologna, fees depend on family income. Politecnico di Milano charges the maximum to non-EU master's students admitted with a degree from abroad, unless they hold a scholarship.
- **Spain:** at UPC, 60 credits for a non-EU, non-resident student come to about €2,700 before administrative fees. EU students pay €17.69 a credit for a qualifying master's.
- **Sweden:** you also pay a SEK 900 application fee for each semester you apply for.

## How do you apply?

Several countries have a national route:

- **Germany:** many universities use uni-assist, which checks applications for over 180 member universities.
- **France:** if you are a citizen of one of 72 listed countries, you pre-register through Études en France. Others apply for the visa through France-Visas.
- **Netherlands:** Studielink is the official portal, but check whether your university uses it. Study in NL gives deadlines of 15 January for numerus fixus programmes and 1 May for other programmes, and says to check your university's date.
- **Italy:** pre-enrol for your course on Universitaly. Only a validated pre-enrolment can support a visa application. For degree courses, the visa deadline is 30 November 2026 for 2026/27, and 31 October 2027 for 2027/28. Universities may set earlier dates.
- **Sweden:** apply on universityadmissions.se. For Uppsala's MSc Computer Science starting in autumn 2027, the deadline is 15 January 2027.

For the general steps, see [how to apply for a master's abroad](/blog/how-to-apply-for-a-masters-abroad/).

## How much can you work, and can you stay after graduating?

Each country sets its own work limit. As of October 2026:

| Country | Work while you study |
|---|---|
| Germany | 140 full days or 280 half days a year, or 20 hours a week in lecture periods |
| France | 964 hours a year |
| Netherlands | 16 hours a week, or full-time in June, July and August, but not both. Your employer needs a work permit |
| Italy | 20 hours a week, up to 1,040 hours a year |
| Spain | Up to 30 hours a week, if the work fits with your studies |
| Sweden | 15 hours a week if your permit was granted from 11 June 2026, with exceptions such as June to August |

The Dutch rule is as the University of Amsterdam explains it. Spain's rule comes from its new immigration regulation, in force since 20 May 2025.

As of October 2026, four of these countries give you time to look for work after you graduate:

- **Germany:** up to 18 months, and you can take any job. The permit cannot be renewed.
- **France:** 1 year, after a master's-level degree or a licence professionnelle from France. It cannot be renewed.
- **Netherlands:** the orientation year lasts 1 year. You can apply for it within 3 years of graduating.
- **Sweden:** up to 1 year after a bachelor's or master's. Apply before your study permit runs out.

For Italy and Spain, check the current job-search rules with the immigration office before you rely on them. To compare more countries, see [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Do you need to speak the local language?

It depends on the programme. At Uppsala University, the 120-credit MSc in Computer Science is taught in English, but the 300-credit Computer and Information Engineering programme requires Swedish. Check the teaching language on each programme page. For English tests, see [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## Rules that depend on your nationality

- Before you have a permit, your nationality decides whether you need a short-stay visa to visit Schengen. Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 lists the countries whose citizens need one, and Annex II those who do not.
- In France, citizens of 72 listed countries go through Études en France.
- For Germany's rules, see [studying in Germany](/blog/study-in-germany/).

For rules linked to your passport, see the guides for students from [India](/blog/students-from/india/), [Pakistan](/blog/students-from/pakistan/), [Nigeria](/blog/students-from/nigeria/), [Egypt](/blog/students-from/egypt/) and [the UAE](/blog/students-from/uae/).

## What to do next

1. Shortlist two or three countries. The guide to [which country you should study in](/blog/which-country-should-you-study-in/) can help.
2. Check the tuition, work and job-search rules for each country on its official pages.
3. Find your application route and deadline: uni-assist, Études en France, Studielink, Universitaly or universityadmissions.se.
4. Work out the money you must show, and any tuition you must pay before the visa.
