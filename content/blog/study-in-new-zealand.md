To study in New Zealand for more than 3 months, you need a student visa, usually the Fee Paying Student visa. As of October 2026, it costs NZ$485 plus levies of NZ$265 and NZ$100, and you must show NZ$20,000 a year for living costs. Since 3 November 2025, eligible students can work up to 25 hours a week. The Post-Study Work Visa can last up to 3 years.

## What kinds of universities and qualifications are there?

Study with New Zealand, run by Education New Zealand, lists eight universities: the University of Auckland, Auckland University of Technology, the University of Canterbury, Lincoln University, Massey University, the University of Otago, Victoria University of Wellington and the University of Waikato.

Qualifications have levels. INZ's post-study work rules refer to a degree at level 7, a postgraduate diploma at level 8, a master's degree at level 9 and a doctoral degree at level 10.

Master's degrees are measured in points, and lengths vary. Canterbury's Master of Engineering is 120 points, and its Master of Applied Data Science is 180 points. The University of Auckland's Master of International Business is "a 15-month, 180-point programme", with 2027 start dates of 12 April and 27 September. Start dates vary by programme, so check yours.

## How do you apply?

1. **Apply to the university.** Choose your programme and check its English requirement. Auckland's Master of International Business, as one example, asks for IELTS Academic 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0.
2. **Pay your tuition.** INZ wants evidence that you have paid your fees "in full for 1 year or 1 programme (whichever is shorter)".
3. **Gather your evidence.** Collect proof of money for living costs and for a ticket home (see below).
4. **Apply for the visa.** Study with New Zealand says you must apply to Immigration New Zealand (INZ) and "have this approved before you're able to start your studies".

## How much does the student visa cost?

INZ's fees guide (May 2026) lists these amounts for a student visa:

| Who applies | Application fee | Immigration levy |
|---|---|---|
| Anyone in New Zealand, and citizens of most countries applying from outside | NZ$485 | NZ$265 |
| Citizens of listed Pacific countries applying from outside New Zealand | NZ$270 | NZ$265 |

INZ also charges the International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy (IVL) on student visas. Its fee table from 1 October 2024 sets the IVL at NZ$100 a person. Pacific citizens are exempt. Check the INZ Fee Finder on the day you apply.

## How much money do you need to show?

As of October 2026, INZ's student fund requirements are:

- NZ$20,000 for each year, if you study for 1 year or more
- NZ$1,667 for each month, if your study is shorter than 1 year
- tuition fees paid in full for 1 year or 1 programme, whichever is shorter
- "a fully paid ticket out of New Zealand" or "enough extra money to buy a ticket"

Evidence INZ accepts includes:

- bank statements with the account holder's name and "the last 3 months of transaction history"
- a sponsor, who "must be a New Zealand citizen or resident, and a family member or friend"
- "an education loan disbursal letter" from a bank, with a sanction letter
- the Funds Transfer Scheme, for students from some countries (see the nationality section)

If you use a loan, INZ may ask how you plan to repay it.

## Do you need insurance?

Plan for it. INZ's Student Visa Paper Application Guide (October 2025) says a student visa may include "the requirement to hold insurance". It adds: "We strongly recommend that you arrange comprehensive health insurance for your stay in New Zealand."

Insurance is an extra cost. Canterbury, for example, says its programme fees do not include insurance.

## How much does it cost to study in New Zealand?

These examples come from each university's own fee page for international students:

| University | Programme | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Canterbury | Master of Engineering, 120 points | NZ$51,900 (2026), NZ$54,600 (2027) |
| Canterbury | Master of Applied Data Science, 180 points | NZ$56,850 (2026), NZ$59,400 (2027) |
| Otago | Master of Business, Business Analytics | NZ$68,145 a year (2027) |
| Otago | MBA, 240 points | NZ$76,240 (2027) |

Canterbury's fees are for the whole qualification, and they do not include its Student Services Levy or insurance.

For living costs, Study with New Zealand quotes university estimates, as of October 2026. Victoria University of Wellington and the University of Auckland estimate NZ$20,000 to NZ$27,000 a year. The University of Otago in Dunedin estimates NZ$18,000 to NZ$21,000.

## Can you work while you study?

Yes, if your visa conditions allow it. From 3 November 2025, INZ raised the limit "from 20 to 25 hours a week" for eligible students. It applies "to all new student visas granted from that date".

If your visa still shows a 20-hour limit, you need a variation of conditions or a new student visa to work 25 hours. INZ gave the variation fee as NZ$325 in its announcement of 21 October 2025.

INZ also says "Most student visas already allow full-time work during summer and other scheduled breaks." Check the conditions on your own visa. To compare countries, see [how many hours international students can work](/blog/student-work-hours-by-country/).

## Can you stay and work after you graduate?

The Post-Study Work Visa lets you stay and work for "up to 3 years", depending on your qualification level and how long you studied for it. As of October 2026, INZ's main rules are:

- a degree at level 7 or higher, or a non-degree qualification on INZ's eligible list
- for a degree, at least 30 weeks of full-time study in New Zealand
- apply "no later than 3 months after your student visa expires" (6 months after a doctorate)
- at least NZ$5,000 for your living expenses
- a fee from NZ$1,670 (NZ$320 plus a NZ$1,350 levy, in the May 2026 guide)

With a degree at level 7 or higher, you can do "any work in any job, anywhere in New Zealand". Your partner can apply for a work visa, and your children for student visas.

INZ has announced two changes that start on 16 November 2026. It will no longer accept paper applications for this visa. Eligibility will also extend to applicants who have "an NZQCF level 7 graduate diploma that they studied full-time for in New Zealand" and "a bachelor's degree that they completed in New Zealand or overseas".

## Can your family come with you?

Possibly. Study with New Zealand says "There are a range of visa options that could allow your family to join you while you study in New Zealand." They include:

- the Partner of a Student Work Visa
- the Partner of a Student Visitor Visa
- the Dependent Child Student Visa and the Child of a Student Visitor Visa

Each one has its own rules, so check the INZ page for the visa that fits your family before you plan the move.

## What are the common mistakes?

- Applying before you have paid a year's tuition, or the whole programme if it is shorter.
- Sending bank statements without 3 months of transaction history.
- Assuming an older visa allows 25 hours. If yours shows 20, you need a variation of conditions.
- Choosing a non-degree course that is not on INZ's eligible list, when you want the Post-Study Work Visa.
- Leaving the Post-Study Work Visa too late. Apply no later than 3 months after your student visa expires.

## Rules that depend on your nationality

- **Funds Transfer Scheme.** INZ lists it for students from China, India, Nepal, the Philippines, Sri Lanka and Vietnam. INZ says using it "does not guarantee approval of your student visa application".
- **Pacific citizens.** Citizens of listed Pacific countries who apply from outside New Zealand pay a lower application fee of NZ$270, and they do not pay the IVL.

## What to do next

1. Choose a programme at level 7 or higher if you want the Post-Study Work Visa, with at least 30 weeks of full-time study in New Zealand.
2. Check the fee for your start year on the university's own page.
3. Gather evidence of NZ$20,000 a year, with 3 months of bank history.
4. Apply for your visa early, and check the fee on the INZ Fee Finder on the day.
