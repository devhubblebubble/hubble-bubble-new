To study for a degree in South Korea you need a D-2 visa. A Korean language course needs a D-4. As of October 2026, the visa fee is about USD 60 for a stay over 90 days. Seoul National University's graduate tuition starts at KRW 3,109,000 a semester. With permission and the right Korean level, you can usually work 25 hours a week in term (30 for graduate students).

## Which visa do you need: D-2 or D-4?

| Visa | Who it is for | Main documents |
|---|---|---|
| D-2 | Degree students at an institution offering an associate degree or higher, and some research | Standard Admission Letter, proof of highest education, proof of financial ability |
| D-4 | Korean language students at a university language institute, and other training | Standard Admission Letter, enrolment or education certificate, proof of financial ability, training plan |

Both visas also need a passport copy and a recent photo. A tuberculosis test may be required. If you use your parents' bank statements, you also need family relationship documents.

A D-2 visa allows a stay of up to 2 years each time it is issued. Overall, you can stay up to 6 years after admission for a bachelor's and up to 5 years for a master's. If you stay more than 90 days, you must complete alien registration within 90 days of arriving.

## How much does a Korean student visa cost?

Study in Korea lists approximate visa fees: about USD 40 for a single-entry stay of 90 days or less, and about USD 60 for 91 days or more. These are approximate, so confirm the fee with the Korean embassy or consulate where you apply.

## How much money do you need to show?

You must provide proof of financial ability for both the D-2 and the D-4. Study in Korea does not give one national figure. Ask your university and the Korean embassy or consulate in your country what amount they expect.

This may change. In October 2026, the Ministry of Justice proposed that applicants with TOPIK level 4 could be exempt from proof of money. It is a plan, and it is not a rule yet.

## How much does it cost to study in Korea?

Fees vary by university and subject. As one example, Seoul National University's Spring 2027 graduate guide lists these fees per semester, based on the 2026 academic year:

| Subject area at SNU | Tuition per semester |
|---|---|
| Humanities and social sciences | KRW 3,109,000 |
| Natural sciences (most departments) | KRW 3,802,000 |
| Engineering | KRW 3,828,000 |
| Fine arts | KRW 4,686,000 |
| Medicine (clinical track) | KRW 5,962,000 |

New students also pay an admission fee of KRW 169,000 in the first semester, and the application fee is KRW 90,000. SNU says its fees may change.

For living costs, Study in Korea's guide puts housing at KRW 500,000 to KRW 700,000 a month and food at KRW 200,000 to KRW 300,000 a month.

## Do you need health insurance in Korea?

Plan for it. As one example, Sungkyunkwan University tells its students that joining National Health Insurance has been mandatory for all international students in Korea since 1 March 2021.

The same page says you are enrolled automatically once your alien registration is complete. For D-2 students, cover starts on arrival or registration. For D-4 students, it starts 6 months after arrival.

## Can you work part-time while studying in Korea?

Yes, with permission. You must get part-time work permission from your local immigration office, and you need a set level of Korean. Talk to your university's international office before you start any job.

Study in Korea's table sets these weekly limits during term, as of October 2026:

| Your course | Korean level needed | Usual limit | Higher limit | Below the Korean level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Korean language course (after 6 months) | TOPIK 2 | 20 hours | 25 hours | 10 hours |
| Associate degree, or bachelor's years 1 and 2 | TOPIK 3 | 25 hours | 30 hours | 10 hours |
| Bachelor's years 3 and 4 | TOPIK 4 | 25 hours | 30 hours | 10 hours |
| Master's or doctorate | TOPIK 4 | 30 hours | 35 hours | 15 hours |

The higher limit is for students at accredited universities, or with excellent grades or strong Korean. Study in Korea's tables show no limit at weekends and in vacations for degree students who meet the Korean level. A line on Study in Korea's employment page says this unlimited allowance does not apply, so confirm it with your university before you work longer hours. Matching levels from the Social Integration Program or the King Sejong Institute also count.

Allowed jobs are usually simple ones, such as restaurant help, office support, tourist guide assistance and duty-free shop sales.

The rules are under review. On 7 October 2026, the Ministry of Justice said it plans to base work permission on Korean ability and grades, raise the Korean requirement to Social Integration Program level 3, and use a confirmation issued by your university. Changes the Ministry can make on its own are planned from the second half of 2026, so check the current rule before you work.

## Can you stay and work in Korea after graduating?

Yes. There are two main routes:

- Job-seeking visa (D-10): for graduates, or expected graduates, of an associate degree from a Korean college, or a bachelor's or higher from a Korean university. You apply at your local immigration office.
- Work visa (E-7): if you graduated from a Korean university with an associate degree or higher, you can change to E-7 without needing work experience, as long as the job relates to your major.

Internships of up to 6 months are also possible. The Ministry of Justice has proposed making the D-10 last 1 to 3 years, depending on Korean ability and job preparation. That proposal is not in force yet.

## Do you need to speak Korean to study in Korea?

For Korean-taught programmes, you usually submit a TOPIK score. TOPIK is the test of Korean for non-native speakers. TOPIK I tests listening and reading, and TOPIK II tests listening, writing and reading. Scores are valid for 2 years.

Some programmes accept English instead. At SNU, as one example, graduate applicants need TOPIK 3, or an English score such as IELTS Academic 6.0 or TOEFL iBT 80 (4.0 for tests taken from 21 January 2026). Some SNU departments ask for TOPIK 5 or 6. SNU names its Global Administration major as taught fully in English, and tells applicants to ask each department about English courses.

Study in Korea's course search lets you filter by language of instruction. Even on an English-taught programme, your Korean level decides how many hours you can work. For test choices, see [which English test you need](/blog/english-tests-for-study-abroad/).

## Can you get a scholarship to study in Korea?

The Global Korea Scholarship (GKS), run by NIIED, covers airfare, Korean language training, tuition and a monthly allowance. Undergraduate scholars do one year of Korean language training before their degree.

- Undergraduate: under 25, with strong grades, for example 80% or higher or the top 20% of your class. Applications open around September.
- Graduate: under 40, with a bachelor's or master's and an average of at least 80%. Applications run from February to March.

You apply through the Korean embassy in your country (embassy track) or directly to a GKS university (university track). You and both your parents must hold non-Korean citizenship. For other options, see [government scholarships for international students](/blog/government-scholarships-for-international-students/).

## Is South Korea a good fit for you?

Korea can suit you if:

- you are ready to learn Korean, which affects your work hours and job options
- you meet the GKS rules and want a funded place
- you want to work in Korea, since graduates can move to E-7 for work linked to their major

Think carefully if:

- you do not want to learn Korean, because many work rights depend on TOPIK
- you need to work long hours, because limits are low below the Korean level
- you need fixed rules, because the Ministry of Justice is reviewing student visas

## What to do next

1. Shortlist programmes for the career you want, and confirm the language of teaching for each one.
2. Start learning Korean early and plan your TOPIK date.
3. Check GKS dates: February to March for graduate study, September for undergraduate.
4. Ask your university how much money you must show and how to get work permission.
