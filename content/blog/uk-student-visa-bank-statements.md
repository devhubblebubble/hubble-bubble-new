For a UK Student visa, you must show your unpaid first-year fees plus money for living costs, as of October 2026. That is £1,171 a month outside London or £1,529 in London, for up to 9 months. From 30 November 2026 these rise to £1,203 and £1,570. The money must be held for 28 days in a row, ending no more than 31 days before you apply.

## How much money do you need to show?

You add two amounts together:

- Your course fees for the first academic year (up to 9 months), minus anything you have already paid. The figure is on your [Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)](/blog/what-is-a-cas/).
- A set amount for each month of your course, up to 9 months.

| Amount | Until 29 November 2026 | From 30 November 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| London, per month | £1,529 | £1,570 |
| Outside London, per month | £1,171 | £1,203 |
| London, 9 months | £13,761 | £14,130 |
| Outside London, 9 months | £10,539 | £10,827 |

London means the City of London and the 32 London boroughs. If your course includes part of a month, it is rounded up to a full month. If family members come with you, you need extra money for each of them.

Here is one example with real figures. Cardiff University's MSc Data Science and Analytics costs £32,450 for international students starting in 2027, with a £3,000 deposit. After paying the deposit, someone applying on or after 30 November 2026 would show £29,450 in fees plus £10,827 for 9 months. That is £40,277 in total.

For the full cost picture, read [How much does it cost to study in the UK in 2026?](/blog/cost-of-studying-in-the-uk/).

## What is the 28-day rule?

You must show that you have held the required money for a 28-day period. gov.uk puts it simply: you must have this money for at least 28 days in a row.

The 28 days are counted back from the closing balance on your most recent statement. Your evidence must cover the whole 28 days. So keep the full amount in the account on every one of those days.

## How recent must the bank statement be?

The end of the 28-day period must be within 31 days of the date you apply. Appendix Finance says your most recent piece of financial evidence must be dated within 31 days before your application.

gov.uk gives this example. If you apply on 1 January, you must show the money was in your account for at least the 28 days ending on 1 December.

## Whose bank account can you use?

The account must be in one of these names:

- Yours, alone or as a joint account holder.
- Your parent's or your legal guardian's.
- Your partner's, if your partner is applying at the same time or already has permission.

If you use a parent's or legal guardian's account, you need proof of the relationship and their written consent to use the money. The person whose name is on the account must control the money.

An account in the name of a brother, sister, uncle or family friend is not on this list. Promises of future support from family or friends are not accepted either.

## Which accounts and banks are accepted?

The money can be in any personal bank or building society account, such as a current, deposit, savings or investment account. gov.uk says you cannot use pensions. The account must let you take the money out immediately.

You cannot use:

- overdrafts or credit cards
- cryptocurrency
- stocks, shares or bonds
- money you cannot withdraw immediately, whatever the notice period

The bank matters too. The money will not be considered if:

- the Home Office cannot make satisfactory checks on it
- the bank is not regulated by the right body in its country
- the bank does not keep electronic records

Money in another currency is converted to pounds at the OANDA spot rate on the date you apply. Keep a margin above the amount in case the rate moves.

## What must the statement show?

You can use bank statements (paper or electronic downloads), building society passbooks, certificates of deposit or letters from your bank or building society. The evidence must show your name, the name of the bank or building society and how much money is in the account.

If you use a parent's account, the statement will be in their name. Send it with the consent letter and the proof of the relationship.

## Do bank statements need to be translated?

Yes, if they are not in English or Welsh. The Student caseworker guidance asks for a fully certified translation from a professional translator or translation company that the Home Office can check. The translation must include:

- confirmation that it is an accurate translation of the document
- the date of the translation
- the full name and signature of the translator, or of an official from the translation company
- the translator's or the company's contact details

## Does money already paid to the university count?

Yes. Course fees you have paid to your university come off the amount you must show. The payment must be on your CAS, or you need a receipt from the university.

An accommodation deposit can also come off, up to £1,529 (£1,570 from 30 November 2026). This only works for accommodation arranged by your university. Payments for other accommodation cannot be taken off.

Your balance may fall well below the amount before the decision. If so, the decision maker must be satisfied the money went on course fees, an accommodation deposit or other costs of your course.

## What if a sponsor or a loan pays?

An official financial sponsor can cover some or all of the money. Appendix Finance lists His Majesty's Government, your national government, the British Council, any international organisation, an international company, a university or an Independent School.

The sponsorship must be stated on your CAS, or you need a letter from the sponsor. The letter shows:

- the date
- the sponsor's name and contact details
- how long the sponsorship lasts
- the amount, or that it covers all your fees and living costs

Money from an official sponsor or a qualifying student loan does not have to meet the 28-day rule. The loan rules are in [Can you use an education loan for a UK Student visa?](/blog/education-loan-uk-student-visa/).

If you completed a UK course in the last 12 months with a government or international scholarship agency paying both your fees and living costs, you also need its written consent for your application.

## Which nationalities do not have to send bank statements?

Some applicants do not need to send financial evidence with their application. This covers British National (Overseas) passport holders and nationals of the countries on the [gov.uk list](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money). Under the rules, you must also be applying from the country where you live, or from the UK.

The list includes Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates. As of October 2026, India, Nigeria, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia are not on it.

Even if you are on the list, you must still hold the money. UK Visas and Immigration can ask for the evidence before it decides your application.

## What are the common mistakes?

- Applying more than 31 days after the closing date on your latest statement.
- Letting the balance drop below the amount during the 28 days.
- Using an account held by a relative who is not your parent or legal guardian.
- Forgetting the parent's consent letter or the proof of the relationship.
- Counting an overdraft, shares or money you cannot take out immediately.
- Sending statements in another language without a certified translation.
- Saving to the old amounts for an application made on or after 30 November 2026.

## What to do next

1. Work out your number: the fees still owed on your CAS, plus the monthly amount for each course month, up to 9.
2. Put the money in an accepted account at least 28 days before you plan to apply, and leave it there.
3. Gather your statement, any consent letter, the proof of relationship and certified translations, then apply within 31 days of the closing balance date.
4. Read [UK Student visa 2026: cost, money and timeline](/blog/uk-student-visa-guide/) for the rest of the application.
