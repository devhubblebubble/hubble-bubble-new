You apply for a student pass through Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS), and get an approval called an eVAL before you travel. As of October 2026, EMGS charges RM1,728 to process an application for university study, plus visa, medical and insurance fees. You can work up to 20 hours a week with approval. Some graduates can then stay 12 months on a Graduate Pass and work part-time.

## What kinds of universities are there in Malaysia?

EMGS lists public universities, private universities, university colleges, private colleges and foreign branch campuses. It says Malaysia has 11 foreign branch campuses "providing the same curriculum, quality, and certification as their main campuses abroad".

That is a general claim, so check each university's own pages. At Monash Malaysia and Southampton Malaysia, most bachelor's degrees take 3 years, and engineering takes 4.

## What degree do you get at a UK or Australian campus?

Here is what four universities say on their own pages, as of October 2026:

| University | What it says about the degree |
|---|---|
| Heriot-Watt University Malaysia, Putrajaya | You graduate with the same Heriot-Watt degree |
| University of Southampton Malaysia, Johor | You graduate with a University of Southampton degree |
| University of Nottingham Malaysia, Selangor | You can earn a UK degree from a Russell Group university |
| Monash University Malaysia | Its home and fee pages do not say |

Nottingham and Monash do not say on these pages whether your certificate is the same as at home. Ask them in writing.

Some campuses offer time at the home campus:

- Heriot-Watt's Go Global scheme lets you transfer to the UK or Dubai for a semester, a year or longer. Longer stays depend on your programme running there.
- Southampton Malaysia mentions a flexible UK transfer programme.
- Monash Malaysia lists a Semester in Australia.
- Nottingham Malaysia says its fixed fee has exceptions if you transfer to the UK campus or join a 2+2 programme.

| University | Programme | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Heriot-Watt Malaysia | BSc Computing Science, 2026/27 | RM51,304 a year |
| Heriot-Watt Malaysia | MBA, 2026/27 | RM73,935 in total |
| Monash Malaysia | Bachelor of Computer Science, 2027 | RM57,600 a year |

| Nottingham Malaysia | BSc Computer Science, from January 2027 | RM59,000 a year |

All three say a 6% service tax applies to non-Malaysian students, so ask whether your quote includes it. Nottingham fixes your fee for the whole programme at the rate for your year of entry. Monash also charges RM100 to apply and RM200 to register. Southampton's computer science page shows a fee for 2023 only, so ask it for the current fee.

## How do you get a student pass?

1. Accept an offer from a Malaysian institution.
2. Apply online through EMGS, or let your institution apply for you. You must be outside Malaysia when the application is submitted.
3. Upload your documents. These include a passport valid for at least 18 months, your offer letter, a health declaration form, your certificates and an English test result.
4. Wait for your eVAL. EMGS aims to process a complete application in 14 working days, and the eVAL is valid for 6 months.
5. Travel to Malaysia. Within 7 working days, do your medical screening at an EMGS panel clinic, or at the university clinic if you are at a public university.
6. Your institution then applies to Immigration to endorse your student pass.

Without an English test, EMGS says processing may be delayed and your pass may not cover your whole course. Without the medical screening, it cannot start your pass endorsement.

## How much does the student pass cost?

EMGS lists these fees, as of October 2026:

| Fee | Amount |
|---|---|
| Processing fee, higher education institution | RM1,728 |
| eVAL | RM159 |
| Student pass | RM60 |
| Visa | Depends on nationality, for example RM50 for India |
| Medical screening | RM250 to RM350 |
| Health insurance, EMGS plans | RM498 to RM644 |
| i-Kad | RM50 |

Since 1 March 2024, an 8% service tax has applied to processing fees for applications through the EMGS website. You also lodge a personal bond, which depends on your nationality. The EMGS document list does not include a bank statement, but you still need to plan for your full tuition and living costs.

## Can you work while you study in Malaysia?

Yes, with conditions. The Immigration Department of Malaysia says:

- Only students at public universities and private higher education institutions can work. Students at language centres, training or skills centres and accredited centres cannot.
- Part-time work is "limited to 20 hours per week, only at approved locations": restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels, and university or college areas.
- You cannot do front desk work or work as a student recruitment agent.
- You must apply first. You need a job offer letter, a copy of your passport, and a support letter from your institution with your semester and holiday dates.
- If you break the rules, your student pass will be revoked.

EMGS says you can work only during semester breaks or holidays of more than 7 days, and not as a cashier. Ask your international office which rule it follows before you accept a job.

The Immigration Department says only master's and PhD students can bring dependants, and dependants cannot work. EMGS says bachelor's students can also apply if they are citizens of listed countries, including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait, Turkey, Iraq and Iran. Check with your institution before you plan to bring family.

## Can you stay and work after you graduate?

Yes, part-time, if your nationality is on the list. The Graduate Pass is for international students who complete a bachelor's degree or higher at a higher education institution in Malaysia. As of October 2026:

- It lasts 12 months and cannot be extended.
- You can work part-time in permitted sectors.
- It costs RM600, plus RM600 for each dependant and a multiple-entry visa fee.
- You apply through EMGS while your student pass is still valid. Immigration says it takes 14 working days once it has a complete application.
- You need a sponsor who is a Malaysian citizen earning at least RM1,500 a month.
- It is open to citizens of 32 countries. For Indian and Chinese nationals, EMGS says it runs until 31 December 2026, case by case, with a letter of good conduct.

## Branch campus or home campus: how do they compare?

| Question | Malaysia branch campus | Home campus in the UK or Australia |
|---|---|---|
| Is it the home degree? | At some universities: check each one | Yes |
| What does the visa cost? | RM1,728 EMGS processing fee, plus the fees above | UK: £558, plus a £776 a year health surcharge |
| Can you work after graduating? | Graduate Pass: 12 months, part-time | UK: 2 years if you apply by 31 December 2026, 18 months from 1 January 2027. Australia: usually 2 to 3 years after a degree |

Study in Malaysia alone does not count towards the UK or Australian visas. For the UK Graduate visa, you must be in the UK when you apply and hold a Student visa. If your course is longer than 12 months, you must have studied at least 12 months of it in the UK. See [UK Graduate visa: 2 years or 18 months](/blog/uk-graduate-visa-18-months-or-2-years/) and [studying in the UK](/blog/study-in/uk/).

For Australia's Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485), you must have held an Australian Student visa in the last 6 months. You also need a recent qualification from a CRICOS-registered course. See [studying in Australia](/blog/study-in/australia/). The guide to [UK and Australian campuses in Dubai](/blog/study-in/dubai/) makes the same comparison for the UAE.

## How do you check a programme is accredited?

The Malaysian Qualifications Agency (MQA) keeps the Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR). It lists accredited programmes institution by institution, and it says MQA monitors the qualifications in it "from time to time". On 22 October 2026, it showed 5,965 accredited programmes.

Find your institution and programme in the register before you apply. If you cannot find it, ask the university for its MQA accreditation details in writing.

## Rules that depend on your nationality

As of October 2026, official pages give these rules by nationality:

- **Visa fee:** RM50 for India, RM15 for Sri Lanka and RM17.20 for Saudi Arabia. It is RM20 for many others, including Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Egypt, Iraq, Iran, the UAE and the UK.
- **Personal bond:** RM750 for India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka. RM2,000 for Nigeria. RM1,500 for Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Iran and every country not named on the EMGS list.
- **Extra documents:** students from Iran, Iraq, Libya, Somalia, Sudan, Syria and Yemen submit every passport page. Sudanese students need an NOC, and Iranian students an LOE from the Iranian Embassy in Kuala Lumpur. Your institution applies for both.
- **Yellow fever:** if you come from a high-risk country, you must show a vaccination card when you enter.
- **Graduate Pass:** India, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Kuwait and the UK are among the 32 countries. Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Turkey, Iraq, Iran and Afghanistan are not.

For more on your country, see the guides for students from [India](/blog/students-from/india/), [Pakistan](/blog/students-from/pakistan/), [Nigeria](/blog/students-from/nigeria/) and [Iran](/blog/students-from/iran/).

## What to do next

1. Check your programme on MQA's Malaysian Qualifications Register.
2. Ask the university, in writing, what your certificate will say and whether its fee includes the 6% service tax.
3. Add up tuition, EMGS fees, insurance and your personal bond for the whole course.
4. If you want to work in the UK or Australia after graduating, compare the home campus too. The guide to [which country you should study in](/blog/which-country-should-you-study-in/) can help.
